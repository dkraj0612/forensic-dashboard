PRESS RELEASE 
 
Biocon’s Pertuzumab Gets Marketing Authorization in EU,  
First Biosimilar Approved Under New Tailored Clinical Approach 
 
• 
EC approval follows recent U.S. FDA approval for the biosimilar  
• 
Aims to broaden treatment options for patients with HER2-positive breast cancer while 
supporting more sustainable healthcare systems in Europe 
• 
The milestone further strengthens Biocon’s growing oncology portfolio and its focus on 
addressing evolving patient and healthcare needs 
 
Bengaluru, Karnataka, India, November 18, 2026 
 
Biocon Limited (BSE: 532523; NSE: BIOCON), an innovation-led global biopharmaceutical company, 
today announced that the European Commission (EC) has granted marketing authorization for 
Pebrilzo®, its biosimilar pertuzumab, for the treatment of HER2-positive breast cancer across multiple 
disease stages in the European Union (EU). 
 
Biocon’s pertuzumab biosimilar is the first biosimilar to receive EC marketing authorisation under the 
European Medicines Agency’s (EMA) new tailored clinical approach. This milestone follows its positive 
CHMP opinion on September 17, 2026. 
 
Shreehas Tambe, CEO & Managing Director, Biocon, said: “The European Commission’s approval of 
Pebrilzo®, our pertuzumab biosimilar, the first under its new tailored clinical pathway, marks a 
significant regulatory milestone for Biocon. With this approval, pertuzumab becomes our fifth oncology 
biosimilar approved in both Europe and the United States, strengthening our global oncology portfolio 
and reinforcing our commitment to expanding access to high-quality, affordable biologic therapies for 
patients with HER2-positive breast cancer.” 
 
Molecule Information: 
Biocon’s pertuzumab biosimilar, approved as a 420 mg concentrate for solution for infusion, is a 
biosimilar medicinal product and the active substance is pertuzumab, a monoclonal antibody that 
binds with high affinity and specificity to the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), 
inhibiting the proliferation of tumour cells that overexpress HER2. 
 
Biocon’s pertuzumab biosimilar is indicated for use in combination with trastuzumab and 
chemotherapy in the neoadjuvant treatment of adult patients with HER2-positive, locally advanced, 
inflammatory, or early-stage breast cancer at high risk of recurrence, as well as in the adjuvant 
treatment of adult patients with HER2-positive early breast cancer at high risk of recurrence. 
 
In addition, it is indicated for use in combination with trastuzumab and docetaxel in adult patients with 
HER2 positive metastatic or locally recurrent unresectable breast cancer, who have not received 
previous anti-HER2 therapy or chemotherapy for their metastatic disease. 
 
Extensive orthogonal, state-of-the-art structural and functional analytical characterisation, together 
with comparative clinical pharmacokinetic data, demonstrated that Biocon’s pertuzumab biosimilar is 
highly similar to the reference product, with no clinically meaningful differences in quality, safety, or 
efficacy.

Biocon in Oncology 
Biocon has a strong and growing commitment to oncology, with a portfolio spanning multiple areas of 
cancer care, including solid tumors such as breast cancer, as well as supportive care addressing 
chemotherapy-induced neutropenia. This commitment also extends to bone health, with therapies 
addressing osteoporosis, treatment-related bone loss and skeletal complications associated with 
advanced cancers1. Biosimilar pertuzumab further strengthens the company’s portfolio and its ability 
to support patients across different stages of the cancer care journey and diverse healthcare systems. 
 
Epidemiology: 
Breast cancer remains the most commonly diagnosed cancer among women in Europe. According to 
2024 estimates from the European Cancer Information System (ECIS), it is the leading cancer diagnosis 
among women across Europe2. Approximately 15–20% of invasive breast cancers are characterised by 
HER2 overexpression or amplification3.Despite significant advances in HER2-targeted treatment, the 
burden of breast cancer underscores the importance of broad and sustainable access to high-quality 
treatment options across European healthcare systems.2,3
