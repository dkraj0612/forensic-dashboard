Detailed Rationale
CRISIL Ratings has assigned its ‘CRISIL A+/Stable’ rating to the long-term bank facilities of Park Medi World Pvt Ltd
(PMWPL; part of the Park group).
 
The rating reflects the Park group’s established market position in North India through 13 hospitals under the Park brand,
stable occupancy rates, healthy average revenue per bed per day (ARPOBD) and sound operational efficiencies leading to
healthy profitability. Operating margin has been robust and is estimated at 30.7% on average in fiscal 2024. The rating also
factors in the promoters’ extensive experience of more than 40 years in the healthcare space where they have built a strong
reputation, the group’s healthy financial risk profile because of strong operating performance, healthy cash generation and
limited leverage despite recent debt-funded acquisitions.
 
These strengths are partially offset by large working capital requirement of the group. Over 85% of the group’s revenue is
from government panels wherein receivables are stretched. However, CRISIL Ratings notes that the receivables position
has improved over the last fiscal, with debtors of over six months reducing to ~Rs 85 crore as on March 31, 2024 (~15% of
total debtors) as compared to Rs 180 crore (~30% of total debtors) as on March 31, 2023. The ratings are also constrained
by exposure to risks related to implementation and timely stabilisation of operations. The group is in the process of setting
up new hospitals in Panchkula and Rohtak, as well as expanding its hospital in Ambala and Jaipur. It has also agreed to
deals for setting up hospitals in Gorakhpur, Lucknow and Narela. CRISIL Ratings expects the group to continue to grow
through brownfield and greenfield expansions over the medium term. Timely stabilisation of new facilities will be a key
monitorable. However, the group’s track record of stabilising previous projects in a timely manner provides comfort.
 
The rating reflects the experienced management and healthy operating capability of the group, its strong financial risk profile
and healthy operating efficiency. These strengths are partially offset by its limited revenue diversity which constrains
business profile, stretched working capital cycle and exposure to risks related to implementation and timely stabilisation of
operations.

CRISIL Ratings has consolidated the business and financial risk profiles of PMWPL and its wholly owned and step-down

Strong financial risk profile: Networth is estimated to be Rs 866.7 crore as on March 31, 2024, with healthy leverage
and comfortable debt protection metrics. The group has a demonstrated track record of maintaining prudence in its
expansion plans as indicated by low gearing. Gearing is estimated at 0.61 time as on March 31, 2024, and total outside

group is likely to undertake further debt-funded greenfield and brownfield expansions of around Rs 100 crore with plans
to set up new hospitals in Panchkula and Rohtak, as well as expanding its hospital in Ambala. It will also set up
hospitals in Gorakhpur, Lucknow and Narela. However, despite this, CRISIL Ratings believes that its capital structure
will be comfortable with the group trying to limit debt in its acquisitions to 60% of total cost. The interest coverage ratio is
estimated at 7.1 times in fiscal 2024 and is expected to be 8-9 times in fiscal 2025, supported by healthy operating
efficiency. 
 
Strong operating efficiency: Despite constant capacity addition, the group has reported above-average operating
profitability of more than 30% over the past four fiscals, benefitting from healthy ARPOBD, revenue per patient and
occupancy levels. The centralised operations at one location and strong brand has enabled the group to report high
profitability. The strong operating performance has also resulted in healthy return on capital employed (RoCE),
estimated at ~30% in fiscal 2024, though it was lower than ~35% in fiscal 2023. CRISIL Ratings expects the group’s
experienced management and strong operating efficiencies to support its business risk profile over the medium term.
 
Weaknesses:
Business profile constrained due to limited diversity in the revenue profile: The group earns over 85% of its
revenue from government panels such as Central Government Health Scheme (CGHS) and Ex-Servicemen
Contributory Health Scheme (ECHS) with cash and third-party administrator (TPA) sales only contributing to 10-14% of
sales over the three fiscals through 2024, leading to high counterparty risk. There have been instances of delayed
receivables from certain panels which has led to the group discontinuing business with them. Therefore, the ability to
sustain its business with regular change leads to constraining the group’s business risk profile.
 
Furthermore, the group, like other hospital chains, remains exposed to regulatory risk. For instance, the performance of
private hospitals was significantly impacted on account of price caps on cardiac stents and knee implants imposed in
the last quarter of fiscal 2017.  Similarly, some hospitals were  affected  by  the  strict bed rationing rules introduced
during the first and second waves of the Covid-19 pandemic. While the group has not been adversely impacted by
these rules, regulatory actions and their impact will remain monitorable. 
 
Stretched working capital cycle: Gross current assets were 251-265 days and are estimated to be above 250 days as
on March 31, 2024, due to receivables of around 156 days. This is because majority of the group’s revenue is from
central government panels, such as CGHS, wherein payments are received in 5-6 months. However, CRISIL Ratings
notes that the receivables position has improved over the last fiscal, with debtors over six months reducing to ~Rs 85
crore as on March 31, 2024, as compared to Rs 180 crore as on March 31, 2023. The working capital cycle is supported
by payables of 120-130 days. With empaneled schemes expected to contribute significantly to the revenue, the working
capital requirement will remain high over the medium term. However, CRISIL Ratings notes that the group has sufficient
liquidity with unencumbered cash of around Rs. 350 crore as on June 11, 2024.
 
Exposure to risks related to implementation and timely stabilisation of operations: The group undertook sizeable
capex (including acquisitions) of around Rs 250 crore in fiscal 2024 and is expected to undertake further capex of ~Rs
100 crore in fiscal 2025. The group is in the process of setting up new hospitals in Panchkula and Rohtak, expanding its
hospital in Ambala and Jaipur and planning to set up hospitals in Gorakhpur, Lucknow and Narela. Timely ramp-up and
stabilisation of operations in the new hospitals will remain key rating sensitivity factors. The ability of the Park group to
set up and stabilise greenfield projects and turn around brownfield projects provides comfort. However, timely
implementation without significant time and cost overruns will be a key monitorable.
Liquidity: Strong
The sanctioned limit of Rs 367 crore was utilised at 34% on average over the 12 months through July 2024. Expected cash
accrual of over Rs 325 crore will comfortably cover yearly debt obligation of Rs 73 crore in fiscal 2025 and Rs 93 crore in
fiscal 2026. The group also has unencumbered cash of around Rs 350 crore as on June 11, 2024, which supports liquidity.
Furthermore, the group maintains sufficient liquidity in each hospital to account for any exigencies in any particular hospital.
Despite plans of greenfield and brownfield expansions over the medium term, CRISIL Ratings expects the group to maintain
its sizeable liquidity over the medium term.
Outlook: Stable
CRISIL Ratings believes the Park group will benefit from the extensive experience of its promoters and established market
position.
