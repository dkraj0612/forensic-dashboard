MANAGEMENT DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS REPORT
INDUSTRY STRUCTURE AND DEVELOPMENTS
The Company’s business primarily comprises trading in Quartz Grit, Powder and related consumables,
along with other raw material trading activities, supported by an investment portfolio. India continues to be
among the leading global producers and exporters of quartz, with Rajasthan remaining the largest quartz-
producing state in the country. Demand for quartz-based products continues to be driven by the construction,
ceramics, foundry and glass industries, and, increasingly, by electronics and solar applications that require
high-purity quartz.
FY 2025-26 was a challenging year for the Company’s trading operations, with revenue from operations at
Rs. 57.38 lakh as against Rs. 157.28 lakh in the previous year, reflecting lower trading volumes during the
year. The Company continues to closely track demand trends across its key end-user segments and
remains focused on rebuilding volumes in the year ahead.
Looking forward, the Company remains committed to exploring new avenues of growth, including diversification
of product offerings and entry into select markets, while remaining mindful of evolving global trade dynamics
affecting the wider quartz industry. With a sound financial base and a clear strategic roadmap, the Company
will continue to focus on rebuilding trading volumes and creating long-term value for its stakeholders.
DISCUSSION ON FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE
This aspect is dealt with in detail in the Directors’ Report.
THREATS AND OPPORTUNITIES
FY 2025-26 was a year of subdued trading activity for the Company, with lower volumes in its raw material
trading segment as compared to the previous year. The Company’s ability to leverage its supply chain
relationships and long-standing client base remains a key strength as it works towards rebuilding volumes
in the coming year.
The Quartz segment of the broader industry continued to see growing domestic demand, with rising adoption
of quartz-based products in housing, commercial and interior applications. During the year, the imposition
of steep US safeguard tariffs (up to 50%) on engineered quartz surface products from India emerged as a
significant headwind for the export-oriented, value-added segment of the Indian quartz industry, particularly
manufacturers of finished quartz slabs and surfaces concentrated in Gujarat, Rajasthan and Telangana.
While the Company’s own business is centred on the domestic Quartz Grit, Powder and raw material
trading segments rather than finished export-oriented products, a slowdown in the export-linked segment of
the value chain could have an indirect moderating effect on overall demand within the Rajasthan quartz-
processing ecosystem, and the Company continues to monitor this development closely.
The Company’s investment portfolio, while providing a stable base of other income through interest and
dividends, remains exposed to market-linked fair value fluctuations, as reflected in the unrealised diminution
recognised through Other Comprehensive Income during the year. The Company continues to review its
investment strategy with a view to balancing risk and return.
Looking ahead, the Company remains focused on rebuilding trading volumes, exploring value-added product
offerings, and evaluating new markets. With a debt-free balance sheet and an agile approach, the Company
is confident of capitalizing on opportunities such as continued domestic construction and infrastructure
activity and the growing use of high-purity quartz in electronics and solar applications, while remaining
watchful of near-term headwinds affecting the broader industry.

assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial statements for

(1)

flows on specified dates that are solely payments of principal and interest on the principal amount
outstanding and selling financial assets.

All the assets and liabilities have been classified as current or non-current as per the Company’s

System of accounting
1)
The Company follows the mercantile system of accounting and recognises income and

adjusted to reflect the impact of changing value in the purchasing power of money.
3)
Estimates and Assumptions used in the preparation of the financial statements and disclosures
are based upon management’s evaluation of the relevant facts and circumstances as of the
date of the financial statements, which may differ from the actual results at a subsequent
date.
2.3      Use of Estimates
The Ind AS enjoins management to make certain estimates and assumptions that affect the amounts
reported in the financial statements and notes thereto. Differences between actual results and
estimates are recognized in the period in which the results are known/materialize.

and the contractual terms of the financial asset give rise on specified dates to cash flows that are
solely payments of principal and interest on the principal amount outstanding.
Financial assets at fair value through other comprehensive income
Financial assets are measured at fair value through other comprehensive income if these financial
assets are held within a business whose objective is achieved by both collecting contractual cash
flows on specified dates that are solely payments of principal and interest on the principal amount
outstanding and selling financial assets.

amortised cost or at fair value through other comprehensive income on initial recognition. The
transaction costs directly attributable to the acquisition of financial assets and liabilities at fair value
through profit or loss are immediately recognised in statement of profit and loss.
