RATING ACTION / OUTLOOK: REAFFIRMATION/ STABLE/ WITHDRAWAL 
Brickwork Ratings (BWR) withdraws the rating for the Tier-II bonds (under Basel III) amounting 
to Rs. 500 Crores (present outstanding nil), on account of full redemption at its maturity, of The 
Jammu & Kashmir Bank Ltd. 
 
BWR also reaffirms the rating at BWR A/Stable for the Additional Tier I bonds (under Basel III) 
amounting to Rs 1,000 Crores of The Jammu & Kashmir Bank Ltd. 
 
The Withdrawal of Rating of Basel III-Tier II Bonds amounting to  Rs. 500 Crores (present 
outstanding nil), was on account of redemption in full upon its maturity in Dec 2024, as conveyed 
by the bank and confirmed by the Debenture Trustee. Withdrawal of Rating is in compliance with 
BWR Rating Withdrawal Policy. 
 
The rating reaffirmation continues to factor in the continued support of the Government of Jammu 
and Kashmir and Ladakh (JKL), as the promoter and major shareholder with a 59.40% stake in 
Jammu & Kashmir Bank as of 30 June 2025. The rating factors in comfortable capital adequacy, 
resource profile, and improved profitability. The rating also factors in the moderate, albeit 
improving, asset quality. Given the strategic importance of the Bank, its operations with regional

KEY COVENANTS OF THE INSTRUMENT/FACILITY RATED:  
 
ISIN INE168A08061: The unsecured listed redeemable non-convertible Basel III Compliant 
Additional Tier I Bonds. These bonds were raised on 14 June 2018 and are perpetual in nature. 
These bonds carry a fixed coupon rate of 10.50% payable on an annual basis from the date of 
issue till its maturity. This bond has a call option on the condition that any Redemption of the 
PDIS on account of exercise of call option shall be subject to the issuer giving not less than fifteen 
calendar days prior notice to the trustee. 
.  
Note on Additional Tier I Bonds under Basel III: The differentiating features of Additional 
Tier-I (AT1) capital instruments (under Basel III) include the discretionary payment of coupons 
by the bank and principal write-down upon the breach of pre-specified triggers. These features 
increase the risk attributes of AT1 instruments over those of Tier-II instruments under Basel III. 
To factor in these risks, BWR notches down the rating on these instruments from the bank's 
Tier-II bonds. The rating on AT1 bonds (under Basel III), therefore, are lowered by at least one 
notch from its Tier-II bonds (under Basel III) rating. The factors based on which the rating of the 
AT1 bonds (under Basel III) is lowered depend on the bank’s asset quality, and its impact on 
profitability and capitalisation. Nevertheless, the bank’s revenue reserves have to be comfortable, 
wherein the bank ensures timely coupon payments.  
 
The factors that could trigger a default event for non-equity Tier-I capital instruments (under 
Basel III) resulting in non-payment of coupon are: i) the bank exercising coupon discretion; ii) 
inadequacy of eligible reserves to honour coupon payment if the bank reports losses or low 
profits; or iii) the bank breaching the minimum regulatory Common Equity Tier-I ratio. Moreover, 
given the additional risk attributes, the rating transition for non-equity Tier-I capital instruments 
(under Basel III) can potentially be higher and faster than that for Tier-II instruments. 
 
The Stable outlook on the Basel III Additional Tier-I bonds is on account of the bank’s adequate 
capitalization levels and reserves. The impact on asset quality and profitability, potentially due to 
lower loan collections and recovery and the resulting impact on provisioning costs over the near 
term along with higher slippages, will be monitorable. The bank’s ability to maintain its capital 
adequacy levels along with improving the asset quality in near to long term will be a key rating 
monitorable.

Key Rating Drivers 
 
Credit Strength-: 
●​ Strong support from the Government of Jammu Kashmir and Ladakh (GoJKL): The 
Government of Jammu Kashmir and Ladakh holds 59.4% stake in the bank as of 30 June 
2025. The bank benefits from the continuing support received from the GoJKL, 
considering its majority shareholding and the importance of the bank in its regions of 
operation in J&K and Ladakh. The Bank holds a dominant position in the Union 
Territories of JKL, constituting the majority of the credit and deposits. Given its high 
systemic importance to the territory’s economy, the Govt of JKL has demonstrated 
consistent need-based support in the past and is expected to do so in the future. The 
Government of JKL has made a provision of Rs. 100 Crore in its Budget 2025-26 for 
capitalization of J&K Bank, regional rural banks, and Cooperative Banks, if required. The 
bank is planning to raise capital through a mix of equity and bonds.  
 
●​ Comfortable resource profile supported by high levels of CASA deposits: Bank has a 
stable and substantial deposit base, with the JKL holding 87.8% of the total deposits. As of 
31 March 2025, total deposits increased by 10.24% YoY, standing at Rs. 148,569 Crores, 
of which term deposits constitute 52.99% of the total deposits of the Bank. The Bank’s 
low-cost CASA deposit has conventionally remained above 50% over the years till March 
2024. However, during the year ended 31 March 2025 and 30 June 2025, CASA ratio 
declined to 47.01% and 45.71% respectively, but was better than peers in the industry. As 
of 30 June 2025, the total deposits of the bank were Rs. 148,542 Crores, CASA ratio was 
45.71% and term deposits were 54.29%.  
 
●​ Comfortable capitalization: The Bank’s capital-to-risk-weighted assets ratio (CRAR) 
stood at 16.29% at the end of FY25 and was 15.33% as at the end of FY24. The bank 
maintained CRAR well above the minimum regulatory requirement of 11.5% (including 
capital conservation buffer of 2.5%). The Tier I Capital adequacy ratio improved to 
13.95% at the end of FY25 as compared to  13.09% at the end of FY24. The Common 
Equity Tier 1 (CET 1) ratio was 12.95% at the end of FY25, an improvement from  
12.02% at the end of FY24. As of 30 June 2025, the total CRAR of the bank was 15.98%, 
Tier I ratio was 13.68% and the Common Equity Tier 1 (CET 1) ratio was 12.69%. 
The bank’s total capital to risk weighted asset ratio, the Tier I ratio and CET-1 ratio 
continued to remain strong and well above the regulatory requirements of a minimum 
Common Equity Tier-1 (CET1) capital ratio of 8.0% (including the capital conservation 
buffer of 2.5%), and a minimum Tier 1 capital ratio of 9.5%, a minimum total capital 
adequacy ratio (total CRAR) of 11.5% as of 30 June 2025.  The Bank also has plans to 
raise equity capital of Rs 1000 Crores over the medium term to maintain healthy 
capitalisation levels. 
 
●​ Improvement in Profitability: Net profit improved by  16.73% y-o-y, Rs. 485 Crores in 
Q1FY26, from Rs. 415 Crores in Q1FY25. The improvement was primarily supported by 
a 28.87% growth in other income and a 9.14% increase in interest income compared to 
Q1FY25. However, due to the creation of One-Time Impairment Provision of Rs. 87 
Crores due to the equity value erosion in Jammu and Kashmir Grameen Bank, following

Credit Risks-: 
●​ Moderation in Asset Quality remains monitorable: The Bank’s asset quality in terms of  
the Gross NPA (GNPA) ratio stood at  3.50% in Q1FY26 (3.91% in Q1FY25; it was 
3.37% in Q4FY25). Advances of the bank declined to Rs.. 104,039 Crs as of 30 Jun 2025 
(Rs. 106,985 Crs as of 31 March 2025 and Rs. 98,580 Crs as of 30 Jun 2024). Net NPA 
was maintained below 1% and stood at 0.82%. The Bank expects the GNPA to improve 
further as it expects better recoveries from some of its major NPAs. Recoveries during the 
Q1FY26 stood at Rs 106 Crores. BWR also notes that the Fresh slippages rose to Rs 274 
Crores in Q1FY26, from Rs 198 Crores in Q4FY25, pushing the slippage ratio above 1%, 
which was maintained below 1% till Q4FY25. The Bank's total SMA book was 15.73% of 
total advances as of 30 June 2025, (it was 21% of total advances as of 31 Mar 2025 and 
26.7% of total advances as of 30 Jun 2024). But, the bank’s SMA 2 book stood at Rs. 3568 
Crs (3.55% of total advances) as of 30 June 2025 , it was Rs 391 Crs (0.38% of total 
advances) as of 31 Mar 2025 and Rs.3085 Crs (3.26% of total advances) as of 30 June 
2024). While the overall reduction in the SMA book is a positive indicator, the volatility in 
the SMA 2 segment warrants close monitoring. BWR shall monitor the recoveries over the 
near to medium term for an improvement in asset quality. 
 
●​ Regional concentration and challenging operating environment: J&K bank has 70.6% 
of its advances and 87.8% of its deposits from J&K as of 30 June 2025,  indicating a

COMPANY PROFILE 
●​ The Jammu and Kashmir Bank Ltd (J&K Bank) is a banking company incorporated in 
1938. The Bank is headquartered in Srinagar, Jammu and Kashmir, India. The bank is the 
first company of Jammu & Kashmir listed on BSE & NSE. The Bank functions as a leading 
bank in the Union Territories of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh (UTs of JKL) and is 
designated by the Reserve Bank of India as its exclusive agent for carrying out banking 
business for the Government of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh. The bank had a 
stakeholding of the Government of UTs of JKL of 59.4% as of 30 June 2025. The JKB 
holds a dominant position in the UTs of JKL, constituting the majority of the credit and 
deposits in the UT. As on 30 June 2025, it operated through a network of 1,019 branches 
and 1424 ATMs. While predominantly focused in J&K, the bank has spread across 20 states 
and 4 UTs. The bank remains largely rural-focused, with 54.4% of the total branches (as on 
30 June 2025) in rural areas, followed by 18.4% in metro, 16.3% in semi-urban, and the 
remaining 10.9% in urban locations.  
 
●​ Mr. Ketan Kumar Joshi was appointed as the new Chief Financial Officer  (CFO) of The 
Jammu and Kashmir Bank Ltd, effective from 27 June 2025, for a period of 3 Years, 
replacing Mr. Fayaz Ahmad Ganai, who was earlier appointed as the bank’s chief financial 
officer (CFO) for three years with effect from 16 April 2024. BWR considers this as a 
rating monitorable, and shall take appropriate action in case of frequent changes in the 
management in a shorter duration. Mr. Ketan Kumar Joshi was previously CFO at North 
East Small Finance Bank Ltd.  He also had international exposure as he worked as CFO at 
Maiwand Bank, Bakhtar Bank and Ghazanfar Bank, Kabul, Afghanistan.
