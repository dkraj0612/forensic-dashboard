Orient Green Power Company Limited (NSE: GREENPOWER | BSE: 533263 | ISIN: INE999K01014), a leading

Andhra Pradesh Electricity Regulatory Commission (APERC) order that had restricted payment for wind energy 
supplied beyond a normative Capacity Utilisation Factor (CUF) of 23%.

A clear ruling on the tariff dispute 
APTEL has rejected the basis on which payments for energy supplied above the 23% normative CUF were 
curtailed. The Tribunal held that normative CUF is a benchmark for tariff determination—not a ceiling on payment. 
Accordingly, energy delivered under the Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) is payable at the applicable PPA tariff even 
where annual generation exceeds the normative CUF. 
The Tribunal has set aside the APERC order, including its cumulative CUF computation and the reduced rate of ₹0.50 
per unit for generation beyond the 23% CUF benchmark. It has directed Southern Power Distribution Company of 
Andhra Pradesh Limited (APSPDCL) to release the withheld amount along with interest, in accordance with the PPA.

Background to the dispute 
BETA owns and operates a 50.4 MW wind power project in Andhra Pradesh. It entered into a 25-year PPA dated 
May 29, 2013 with APSPDCL, at the APERC-determined tariff of ₹4.70 per unit, with the project commissioned on July 
27, 2013. 
APSPDCL withheld ₹5.93 crore for 1,26,17,939 units supplied between July 27, 2016 and July 27, 2017, treating the 
units as generation beyond the 23% CUF benchmark. BETA approached APERC for release of the amount. APERC’s 
order dated September 26, 2022 limited the ₹4.70 per-unit tariff to generation up to a cumulative CUF of 23% and 
provided for ₹0.50 per unit beyond that level. BETA appealed to APTEL, while APSPDCL filed a cross-appeal; the 
matters were heard together with related appeals. 
Management commentary 
“The favourable order passed by the Appellate Tribunal for Electricity in the matter concerning our material 
subsidiary, Beta Wind Farm Private Limited, marks an important development in the recovery of long-pending 
receivables. The Tribunal has set aside the earlier order of APERC and directed APSPDCL to release the 
withheld payments along with applicable interest, reaffirming the applicability of the agreed tariff for all 
electricity supplied under the Power Purchase Agreement, irrespective of generation exceeding the normative 
CUF of 23%. The total receivable is approximately ₹12.98 crore, including principal and accumulated interest. 
 
We remain focused on pursuing the implementation of the Tribunal’s order and recovery of the outstanding 
amount. This development provides greater clarity on the tariff-related dispute and is expected to support the 
material subsidiary’s cash flows upon receipt of the amount.” 
— Mr. T. Shivaraman, Managing Director & CEO 
About Orient Green Power Company Limited 
Orient Green Power Company Limited (OGPL), headquartered in Chennai and promoted by SVL Limited, is among 
India’s largest independent renewable power producers. The Company operates approximately 381.7 MW of wind 
power capacity across Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat and Karnataka, a 10.5 MW wind farm in Croatia, Europe, 
and a 24.6 MW solar power plant in Tamil Nadu. 
As an independent operator and developer of wind farms in India by aggregate installed capacity, OGPL focuses on 
identifying optimal locations, deploying advanced wind turbine designs and maximising generation efficiency. In FY26, 
the Company reported consolidated total income of ₹315.57 crore, EBITDA of ₹205.45 crore and net profit of ₹71.57 
crore.
