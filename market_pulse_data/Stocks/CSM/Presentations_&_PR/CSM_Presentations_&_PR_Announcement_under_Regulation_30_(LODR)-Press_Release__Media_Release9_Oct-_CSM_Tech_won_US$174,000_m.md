CSM Tech Limited  Wins TradeMark Africa Mandate for Beira Corridor 
 
US$174,000 project to build a digital platform for real-time trade and transport 
monitoring in Mozambique 
 
Bhubaneswar / Nairobi 
October 9, 2026 
 
CSM Tech Limited, Wholly Owned subsidiary of CSM Technologies Limited (NSE: CSM 
| BSE: 544806), a multinational GovTech company delivering Digital Public 
Infrastructure (DPI) solutions across emerging economies, has secured a US$174,000 
(approximately ₹1.65 crore) mandate from TradeMark Africa (TMA) for the design, 
development and deployment of the Mozambique Transport and Trade Observatory 
(TTO) for the Beira Corridor. 
 
The project will be executed by CSM Tech Limited, and will create a centralised digital 
platform to provide corridor authorities and stakeholders with near real-time visibility 
into cargo movement, transport performance, delays, bottlenecks and other trade 
indicators. 
 
The Beira Corridor is a strategic trade route connecting the Port of Beira in Mozambique 
with Zimbabwe and extending trade links to Malawi, Zambia and the Democratic 
Republic of Congo (DRC). It is particularly important for land-linked economies that 
depend on efficient access to regional and international markets. 
 
The need for better data and visibility is underscored by a TradeMark Africa-supported 
diagnostic study conducted in March 2026 involving more than 1,000 truck drivers. The 
study found that while travel times along parts of the corridor could average six to seven 
hours, performance varied significantly, with delays concentrated around the Port of 
Beira and Machipanda border. Baseline transport costs were estimated at US$1,600–
1,960 per trip but could rise to US$4,000 or more than US$6,000 under high-delay 
scenarios, highlighting the economic impact of bottlenecks and non-tariff barriers.

The new Observatory is designed to address this data and coordination challenge by 
creating a single, evidence-based view of corridor performance. 
 
CSM Tech Limited will design and deploy a secure, web-based and microservices-
oriented platform featuring interactive KPI dashboards, GIS-based corridor mapping, 
automated alerts, reporting and data analytics. The system will integrate priority 
information from port, road, rail, customs and other relevant trade and transport 
systems across Mozambique and neighbouring countries. 
 
A centralised data warehouse, supported by a four-stage data validation workflow, will 
help improve the reliability and consistency of information available to corridor 
stakeholders. The solution will also be hosted within Mozambican data centres, 
supporting data sovereignty and applicable local data protection requirements. 
 
The engagement covers requirements validation and detailed solution design, agile 
development and systems integration, testing, stakeholder training, a pilot 
implementation along the Beira–Machipanda route, production deployment and 
handover. CSM will subsequently provide 12 months of support and maintenance under 
a Service Level Agreement. The overall assignment is expected to run for approximately 
18 months from signing. 
 
Commenting on the engagement, Priyadarshi Pany, Managing Director & CEO, 
CSM Technologies Limited, said: 
 
“The Beira Corridor is a vital economic artery for Southern Africa, and its efficiency 
depends increasingly on the quality and timeliness of information available to decision-
makers. We are privileged to partner with TradeMark Africa on the Transport and Trade 
Observatory and to bring our GovTech experience to a platform that can provide 
corridor institutions with a shared, data-driven view of how trade moves. Our objective 
is to make complex trade and transport data actionable, helping stakeholders identify 
bottlenecks earlier, strengthen coordination and make evidence-based decisions.” 
 
“Trade corridors generate enormous amounts of operational data, but its value is limited 
when information remains fragmented across institutions and systems. The Beira 
Corridor Observatory will bring these data streams together to create a common 
operating picture for stakeholders. By combining data integration, GIS, analytics and 
automated alerts, the platform can help transform corridor monitoring from a largely 
reactive exercise into a more proactive and evidence-led process.” 
 
The assignment comes at a time when African governments and regional institutions 
are placing greater emphasis on digital trade facilitation and data-driven corridor

The World Bank has similarly identified the Beira and Nacala corridors as important

The Observatory assignment also builds on CSM Technologies’ growing engagement 
with TradeMark Africa and African governments. Earlier this year, CSM Technologies 
delivered and launched Kenya’s Electronic Warehouse Receipt System (eWRS) and 
Central Registry Platform in partnership with the Warehouse Receipt System Council 
and TradeMark Africa. 
 
The Beira Corridor engagement further builds on CSM Technologies’ experience in 
developing large-scale digital platforms for trade facilitation, logistics, natural-resource 
governance and regulatory administration across India and Africa. 
 
In India, CSM Technologies has implemented major digital mining and mineral-
administration platforms for state governments, including RajMines in Rajasthan, i3MS 
in Odisha and Khanij Online in Chhattisgarh, among others. RajMines has facilitated 
more than 20.5 million digital transit permits, according to CSM Technologies. 
 
The company recently expanded its African GovTech portfolio with the deployment of 
Kenya’s nationwide Royalty Management System through CSM Tech Limited. The 
platform provides digital capabilities for royalty declaration, assessment, payment and 
monitoring for Kenya’s mining sector. 
 
The Beira Corridor project represents another step in the internationalisation of Indian-
built GovTech capabilities, demonstrating how digital platforms developed from India’s 
experience in large-scale public-sector transformation can be adapted to address 
complex governance, trade and infrastructure challenges in emerging economies.
