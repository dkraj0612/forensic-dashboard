PRESS RELEASE 
 
 
DECCAN GOLD'S BHALUKONA PROJECT SHOWS POTENTIAL FOR 4 MILLION 
TONNES OF NICKEL-COPPER-PGE MINERALISATION 
 
• 
Preliminary modelling indicates an estimated grade of 0.24% nickel equivalent at the 
Bhalukona, Chhattisgarh project. 
• 
Current estimates cover just 25% of the identified 1.3-km mineralised corridor, with 
further exploration potential. 
• 
15 drill holes covering 2,773 metres establish a promising geological footprint for India's 
critical-minerals ambitions. 
 
Chhattisgarh/Bengaluru, Nov 1, 2026: Deccan Gold Mines Limited (BSE: 512068), India's first and 
only listed gold and critical-minerals resource company, has reported an encouraging milestone at its 
Bhalukona Nickel-Copper-Platinum Group Elements (Ni-Cu-PGE) project in Chhattisgarh, with 
preliminary geological modelling of exploration drilling data indicating the potential for over 4 million 
tonnes of mineralisation grading approximately 0.24% nickel equivalent (Ni_Eq). 
 
Significantly, the preliminary estimates cover only near-surface mineralisation across approximately 300–
350 metres of the 1.3-km mineralised corridor identified at Bhalukona. They do not account for potential 
repetitions of the mineralised sequence indicated by drone magnetic and induced polarisation (IP) surveys, 
suggesting further exploration potential across the project area. 
 
The modelling represents an important step in understanding the scale and geological characteristics of the 
Bhalukona deposit. The estimated nickel-equivalent grade is based on the weighted contribution of nickel, 
copper, platinum, and palladium mineralisation. 
 
Commenting on the development, Dr. Hanuma Prasad Modali, Managing Director, Deccan Gold 
Mines Limited, said: “India's critical-minerals ambitions will ultimately be determined not just by the 
resources we know we possess, but by our ability to discover, evaluate and responsibly develop them. 
Bhalukona is an encouraging milestone in our effort to unlock India's domestic critical-mineral potential. The 
preliminary modelling points to a promising nickel-copper-PGE system, with the current estimates covering 
only a fraction of the identified mineralised corridor. India's resource security will depend on our ability to 
discover and responsibly develop strategic mineral resources at home. Our focus is to advance Bhalukona 
through science-led exploration, technical rigour, and long-term commitment, contributing to a stronger 
domestic mining ecosystem and more resilient critical-mineral supply chains.” 
 
Systematic exploration establishes a promising geological footprint. 
Deccan Gold was among the early companies to receive a nickel exploration licence in India, with the 
Government of Chhattisgarh executing the Bhalukona–Jamnidih Composite Licence in favour of the 
Company on April 1, 2025. 
 
Covering approximately 30 sq. km in Basana Tehsil, Mahasamund District, Chhattisgarh, the project has 
undergone a staged exploration programme over the past 19 months. The programme has included drone 
magnetic and LiDAR surveys, deep IP surveys, electrical resistivity tomography, geological mapping, rock-
chip and channel sampling, and bulk sampling for beneficiation studies. Since March 2026, the Company 
has also undertaken a diamond-core drilling programme comprising 15 drill holes totalling 2,773 metres.

The exploration work has identified a layered mafic-ultramafic geological formation hosting nickel-copper-
PGE sulphide mineralisation. Drilling has established a mineralised corridor more than 100 metres wide, 
extending approximately 1.3 km in length and to depths exceeding 200 metres. 
 
The mineralisation includes disseminated as well as heavy-to-massive sulphide occurrences, with the 
principal sulphide minerals identified as pyrrhotite, pentlandite and chalcopyrite. These are associated with 
nickel and copper mineralisation, alongside palladium and localised platinum occurrences. 
 
Further exploration to assess the full potential. 
The preliminary geological interpretation provides a strategic direction for the Company's next phase of 
exploration. The current model covers only approximately 25% of the identified mineralised corridor and 
does not include potential repetitions of the mineralised sequence suggested by geophysical surveys. 
 
The Company will continue to evaluate the geological data and use the results of its exploration programme 
to guide further work and assess the extent and continuity of the mineralised system. 
 
The figures represent preliminary exploration-stage interpretations and do not constitute a defined 
mineral resource or mineral reserve. Further exploration, geological evaluation, and technical assessment 
will be required to establish the scale, continuity, and economic potential of the mineralisation.
