Apollo Micro Systems Limited (AMS) (NSE: APOLLO | BSE: 540879) today announced that it has been formally 
shortlisted and empanelled by the Indian Air Force (IAF) as a Prime Development Agency (DA) for the Indigenous 
Precision Range Extension Kit (IPREK) programme, under the Make-II (Industry Funded) category of India's Defence 
Acquisition Procedure 2020 (DAP-2020). This empanelment marks AMS's entry into India's precision guided 
munition (PGM) segment as a Prime one of the highest-value, highest-barrier segments of the Indian defence 
manufacturing market. 
 
What is the Make-II Programme? 
The Make-II (Industry Funded) sub-category is one of India's most progressive defence procurement pathways 
under DAP-2020. Indian companies are invited to invest in the design and development of advanced military 
systems at their own cost, with the Government committing to procure successful prototypes at scale.  
For Apollo Micro Systems, this empanelment is more than a programme win. It is the Indian Air Force's formal 
endorsement of AMS as the company capable of designing, developing, and delivering a world-class precision 
guided weapon system entirely from Indian soil. 
 
What is the IPREK? 
IPREK is a bolt-on precision guidance and range extension kit designed to transform India's existing inventory of 
General Purpose (GP) 500 Kg unguided bombs into long-range precision glide weapons. A weapon fitted with the 
IPREK can be released from over 80–100 kilometres away and still land within 3 metres of its intended target 
placing the carrier aircraft well beyond the reach of most air defence systems. The IPREK converts India's existing 
bomb inventory into a precision standoff capability at a fraction of the cost of procuring new imported precision 
weapons.

systems in the world to use the Indian satellite constellation at its core. It also incorporates Electro-Optical (EO) 
and Infrared (IIR) terminal guidance for day/night precision, and DSMAC (Digital Scene Matching) terminal 
guidance for GPS-denied operations.

The IPREK programme is strategically significant across three dimensions: 
 
• India currently imports the bulk of its precision guided munitions. IPREK directly displaces that import 
dependency the IAF's existing bomb inventory becomes a strategic asset rather than a sunk cost. 
• Precision standoff capability is a force-multiplier: a weapon that can be released from 100 km away keeps 
the carrier aircraft and crew safe from air defences, fundamentally changing mission planning and risk 
calculus. 
• With 500+ IPREK units in the initial procurement phase and further demand across all IAF platforms IPREK 
represents a durable, multi-year revenue opportunity for AMS with strong margin characteristics.
