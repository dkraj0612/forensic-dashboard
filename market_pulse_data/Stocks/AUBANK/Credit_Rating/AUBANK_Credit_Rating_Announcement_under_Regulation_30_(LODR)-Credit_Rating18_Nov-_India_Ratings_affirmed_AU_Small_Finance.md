Analytical Approach
Ind-Ra continues to take a standalone view of AU SFB to arrive at the ratings.
Detailed Rationale of the Rating Action
The rating reflects AU SFB’s fast-growing franchise, above-average profitability and adequate capital buffers. AU SFB
benefits from its long operating track record and strong expertise in commercial retail assets, while gradually expanding
into newer products. Adequate provisioning against stress in unsecured segments provides some cushion to asset quality,
although the impact of weather-related factors and the interest-rate environment remains monitorable. The transition to a
Universal Bank could strengthen its deposit franchise and brand recognition, lower funding costs over time, and provide
regulatory benefits, including lower capital and priority-sector lending requirements.
However, its business profile remains constrained by limited asset diversification, with auto and micro-small and medium
enterprises (MSME) loans still dominating the portfolio, alongside geographic concentration. While liability granularity has
improved, reliance on bulk deposits remains relatively high, resulting in its cost of funds remaining above peers. The bank’s
ability to realise these liability-side benefits while containing operating costs remains a key rating monitorable. The bank’s
granular, higher-yielding retail asset franchise partly offsets the higher funding costs, although diversification into gold and
unsecured lending remains modest. The bank’s medium-term net interest margins (NIMs) could moderate in medium term,
given that higher proportion of the loan book at fixed rate.

Detailed Description of Key Rating Drivers
Well-Positioned Retail Asset Franchise: AU SFB is the largest small finance bank (SFB) in India in terms of assets. The
bank has a demonstrated track record of three decades of catering to the unorganised customer segment, which has
enabled it to maintain a balance between profitability and asset quality across economic cycles. AU SFB has a well-defined
strategic business unit structure, enabling a clear focus on product segments, along with teams that possess local
knowledge across operating geographies. However, its business profile remains constrained by limited asset diversification,
with auto and MSME loans dominating the portfolio, alongside continuing geographic concentration in Rajasthan, though
share of its home state’s gross loan portfolio moderated to 25% in 2QFY27 (FY23: 35%, FY18: 54%). 
Scaling Secured Products for Market Leadership: The bank’s portfolio largely comprising retail secured portfolio
(65.6%), commercial banking (23.0%), unsecured (7.5%), and others (3.9%). AU SFB had been operating in the retail
secured segments and commercial segment even before turning into a bank. At end-2QFY27, its loans grew 25% yoy or
7% qoq to INR1.54 trillion, led by strong growth in auto loans (24% yoy /7% qoq ), MFI (23% yoy /6% qoq) and commercial
vehicles (CV; 30% yoy /6% qoq), while mortgage growth remained moderate at 12% yoy/2% qoq amid management’s
calibrated approach to affordable housing. The management expects mortgage growth of 13%-14% in FY27, accelerating
to about 20% from FY28 as the southern and western franchises scale up. Ind-Ra expects the bank to continue to grow its
loan book at above-industry rates due to its smaller scale, and it is in better position to grow compared to non-banking
financial companies (NBFCs) due to the cost of fund advantage.
The bank also has a competitive advantage in terms of reach and local knowledge in Rajasthan. In addition, it has a
reasonable presence in Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra, which, along with Rajasthan, accounted for 53% of the loan
portfolio in 2QFY27 (2QFY26: 55%; FY24: 62%). While this creates geographical concentration risk, the contribution of the
south region stood 12% at end-2QFY27 (2QFY26: 10%); moreover, the bank has been focusing on expanding its franchise
in newer geographies such as Uttar Pradesh, east India and southern markets, as well as broadening its product
offerings.  
Healthy Capital Base with Earnings Resilience: AU SFB has a demonstrated track record of equity capital raising along
with maintaining healthy internal accruals. During FY23, AU SFB raised INR20 billion of equity capital through the qualified
institutional placements and INR7.7 billion through Tier-II bonds. At end-2QFY27, the bank’s capital buffers remained
adequate, with shareholder funds of INR218.1 billion and capital adequacy ratio and Tier -1 ratio of 18.5% and 16.8%,
respectively (FY26: 18.7%/16.9%, FY25: 20.1%/18.1%). 
Profitability buffers Stable: AU SFB primarily focuses on the granular retail segment and MSME segment, which carry
higher yields compared with its peers, but remain sensitive to higher interest rate environment and weather-related
conditions. The bank’s average cost of funds decreased to 6.1% in 1HFY27 (1HFY26: 6.7%) due to the cut in deposit rates
in FY26 and inflow of FCNR(B) deposits of USD230 million, which helped bank to maintain a healthy net interest margin
(NIM) of 5.8% in 2QFY27 (2QFY26: 5.5%).  Although the current profitability remains healthy with ongoing return on assets
(ROA) of 1.7% in 1HFY27 (FY26: 1.6%), margin pressure could persist, given the predominantly fixed-rate loan book
(about 70%), limiting the ability to immediately pass on higher funding costs and repricing benefits should emerge with a
lag, as floating-rate assets (about 30%) reset over three-to-six months, while higher yields on incremental disbursements
should partly cushion funding-cost pressures.
On the liability side, to build and sustain the liability franchise, the bank needs to spend upfront on operating investments to
create brand awareness. Its operating expenses remain elevated due to continued technology investments, which account
for around 25% of expenditure. As a result, the cost-to-income ratio of the bank remained elevated at 57.7% in 2QFY27
(FY26: 57.9%; FY25: 56.5%). AU SFB had a healthy pre-provision operating profit (PPOP) buffer (PPOP/provision cost) of
4.2x in 2QFY27 (FY26:3.2x, FY25: 2.6x). AU SFB has adequate PPOP and capital adequacy buffers to absorb incremental
asset quality pressures in a stress case scenario. Although potential regulatory changes affecting insurance fee income
pose additional headwinds, growing contributions from forex, trade finance, credit cards, processing fees and transaction
banking should partly diversify fee income. 
Asset Quality Stable, but Economic Sensitivity Remains Monitorable: AU SFB lends to self-employed individuals who
run small businesses, which are largely earn-and-pay in nature. Hence, the cash flows of such borrowers remain
vulnerable to external economic shocks, inflationary pressures on ground and amid higher interest rate environment over
medium term. At end-September 2026, 92.5% of the assets were secured in nature, based on the products.

AU SFB’s asset quality continued to improve, with gross non-performing assets (NPA) declining 42bp yoy to 1.99% and net
NPA falling 7bp yoy to 0.81%, despite a 31bp qoq increase in slippages to 2.66%, primarily due to seasonal stress in the
tractor portfolio; slippages in the unsecured segments, however, moderated. Stress in unsecured segments has largely
normalised, particularly across the microfinance institutions (MFIs) and credit card portfolios, contributing to lower credit
costs, though the provision cover ratio (PCR, ex technical write-offs) at 59.7% in 2QFY27 remained lower than peers. Risks
in the MFI portfolio remain well mitigated, with around 99% of the portfolio covered under the credit guarantee fund for
micro units (CGFMU) guarantee. Asset quality across the retail and commercial portfolios also remains resilient.
Nevertheless, potential asset-quality pressures arising from deficient rainfall and drought conditions remain monitorable
over the near term. H1FY27 credit costs stood at around 73bp, below the FY27 guidance of 90bp, providing some buffer to
absorb potential one-off stress during the remainder of the year. Ind-Ra will monitor steady-state asset quality trends with
adequate vintage in the unsecured segment.
Liability Franchise relies on Wholesale Funding: The bank’s deposit franchise remained supported by stronger
mobilisation efforts led by USD230 million raised through FCNR(B) deposits. However, the current account savings account
(CASA) ratio remained modest at 29.5% in 1HFY27 (FY26: 28.4%, FY25: 29.2%; FY24: 33.4%), driven by the overall
industry trend of a shift in CASA towards term deposits due to the attractiveness of high rates offered to the depositors.
More importantly, granular retail liabilities franchise strength (CASA+ retail term deposit / total deposit) remain weak at
58.5% in 1HFY27 (FY26: 57.3%; FY25: 61.6%) and the bank continue to depend on relatively higher share of bulk deposits
at 38.0% in 1HFY27 (FY24: 33.5%), though it remains compensated by higher yields on its retail product portfolio. This high
share remains moniotorable for asset liability mismatch, which could be triggered during tightened liquidity environment.
The share of the 20 largest depositors to the total deposits of the bank stood at 13.33% in FY26 (FY25: 11.75%, FY24:
10.8%).
Over the longer term, AU SFB’s transition to a universal bank is expected to strengthen brand recognition and facilitate
greater diversification of its retail liability franchise, potentially supporting lower funding costs. The transition could also
provide regulatory benefits through lower minimum capital requirements and priority-sector lending obligations, thereby
supporting growth. However, deposit pricing will remain influenced by industry dynamics and competitive intensity, while
stronger brand recognition should gradually reduce customer acquisition costs. The bank’s ability to realise these liability-
side and regulatory benefits remains a key rating monitorable.
Liquidity
Adequate: AU SFB operated with gap of 9.1% (of total inflows) in asset-liability tenors in the up-to-one-year bucket as of
September 2026. The bank largely covers the gap with rollover of deposits and refinancing lines. It maintains adequate
refinance funding lines from refinancing institutions at any given point in time. The bank also benefits from access to
liquidity support mechanisms such as the liquidity adjustment facility, marginal standing facility, and the call money market,
enabling it to efficiently manage short-term liquidity requirements. During 2QFY27, the bank had a quarterly average
liquidity coverage ratio of 116%, which is above the regulatory requirement (100% for small finance banks).

About the Company
AU SFB is a scheduled commercial bank that commenced its operations as an SFB on 19 April 2017. As on 30 September
2026, the bank was the largest SFB in terms of gross advances and deposits. The bank had a total asset base of INR2,118
billion at end-September 2026, with 3,103 touchpoints across 21 states and four union territories, supported by a workforce
of 60,947. The bank has shareholder funds of INR218.1 billion, deposit base of INR1,704.6 billion and gross advances of
INR1,502.9 billion. AU SFB is listed on the National Stock Exchange of India Limited and BSE Ltd. The bank has received
in-principle approval from the RBI for transitioning to universal bank.
