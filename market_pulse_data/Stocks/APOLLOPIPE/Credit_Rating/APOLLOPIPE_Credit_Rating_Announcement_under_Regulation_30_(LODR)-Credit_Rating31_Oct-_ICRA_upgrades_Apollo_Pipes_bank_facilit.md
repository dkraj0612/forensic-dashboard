Rationale 
The upgrade in the ratings assigned to the bank lines of Apollo Pipes Limited (APL/the company) factors in the strengthening of 
its financial flexibility through a deeper integration with the APL Apollo Group, led by Mr. Sanjay Gupta. With Mr. Sanjay Gupta, 
the Managing Director and Chairman of APL Apollo Tubes Limited {APL Apollo, rated [ICRA]AA+ (Stable)/[ICRA]A1+} taking over 
as the Chairman of APL and increasing his ownership, ICRA expects the company to benefit from a stronger alignment with the 
Group. The integration is expected to enhance APL’s market reach through access to the distribution and dealer network of APL 
Apollo Tubes Limited, supporting a faster growth ramp-up, particularly in the southern and western markets where it has a 
limited presence. ICRA also expects the company’s access to bank funding to further improve through common banking 
channels. The ratings also derive comfort from the commitment from the promoters to provide timely operational and funding 
support to the company in the event of any unforeseen liquidity constraints to ensure timely debt servicing by APL. 
APL’s performance moderated in FY2026, with the revenue declining by ~6% year-on-year (YoY) due to lower realisations 
following a drop in poly-vinyl chloride (PVC) resin prices, while the consolidated volume rose by a modest 4% YoY. The demand 
conditions remained subdued across key end-user segments, impacted by muted government spending and weak traction in 
the real estate sector. The performance witnessed a sharp correction in Q1 FY2027, with the revenue growing by around 7% 
YoY, largely driven by the increase in PVC prices in April 2026 while the volumes grew by ~3% YoY.  
The operating profitability, however, remained under pressure, with the OPBDITA moderating to Rs. 72.1 crore in FY2026 from 
Rs. 97.1 crore in FY2025 due to continued weak performance of Kisan Mouldings Limited, the company’s strategy to protect 
market share through price actions and the volatility in PVC prices. The profitability weakened further in Q1 FY2027, with the 
operating margin declining to 1.0% owing to sharp inventory losses amid a sharp volatility in PVC prices and initial ramp-up costs 
associated with the commissioning of the Varanasi plant. The operating profitability is expected to gradually recover hereafter, 
supported by stable PVC prices, ramp-up of the Varanasi plant and scaling up of its ceramics business. 
The ratings continue to factor in APL’s established position in the domestic plastic pipes industry under the APL Apollo brand, 
supported by its extensive product profile and distribution network. The company’s manufacturing units are located across 
India which enables it to cater to a pan-India distribution network. The Varanasi facility has commenced operations recently 
and should strengthen APL’s presence in the eastern part of the country. Following the integration with APL Apollo’s distribution 
network, the company’s marketing reach will expand further.  
APL has also expanded into value-added products such as bathroom fittings, water tanks, oPVC pipes and uPVC doors and 
window profiles. All the three O-PVC lines are operational, although the revenue contribution remains modest owing to the long 
approval cycles and a gradual build-up of the order book. The doors and windows business, which was commercially launched 
in June 2025, also remains in the scale-up phase. Additionally, the company has forayed into ceramic tiles manufacturing and

benefits which will support the cash flow generation from operations and keep the credit metrics stable. 
 
Key rating drivers and their description 
 
Credit strengths 
 
Established position in domestic PVC pipes industry; strong dealership network and strong financial flexibility – APL 
manufactures and markets uPVC, cPVC, HDPE and oPVC pipes and fittings, besides allied products such as water tanks, bath 
fittings, solvent cement and uPVC doors and window profiles. The company shares the APL Apollo brand with APL Apollo Tubes 
Limited, a leading player in the ERW pipes segment, which supports its brand recognition, particularly in North India. Further, 
APL’s position is supported by a pan-India distribution network of over 1,000 channel partners. While North India remains its 
key market, the acquisition of Kisan Mouldings and commissioning of the Varanasi facility have widened its manufacturing 
presence across western, central and eastern India. However, the benefits of the expanded footprint remain contingent on the 
ramp-up in volumes and utilisation across these facilities. 
Pan-India manufacturing presence and diversified product profile – The company has manufacturing facilities at Dadri and 
Sikandrabad in Uttar Pradesh, Ahmedabad in Gujarat, Tumkur in Karnataka and Raipur in Chattishgarh. Its Varanasi facility has 
commenced operations recently and is currently in the ramp-up phase. Kisan Mouldings has manufacturing facilities at 
Tarapur/Mahagaon in Maharashtra and Silvassa in Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu. 
APL had started out as a North India-based player and has gradually expanded its presence in other geographies through organic 
and inorganic routes, positioning itself well to serve new markets over the long term. Further, the geographical diversification 
beyond North India provides better insulation from regional demand-supply dynamics. The company has an expansive product 
profile comprising uPVC, cPVC, HDPE and oPVC pipes and fittings, catering to the agriculture, housing, water management and 
infrastructure segments. It has also ventured into value-added products such as bathroom fittings, water tanks and uPVC doors 
and window profiles, which have relatively lower seasonality than traditional PVC pipes. 
Comfortable capital structure and coverage indicators - APL’s capital structure remained comfortable, with total debt/TNW of 
0.14 times as on March 31, 2026 compared with 0.10 times as on March 31, 2025, supported by a consolidated tangible net 
worth, including minority interest, of Rs. 893.0 crore. The consolidated debt increased to Rs. 123.4 crore as on March 31, 2026, 
from Rs. 91.8 crore on March 31, 2025, primarily due to higher short-term borrowings. The cash and liquid investments 
moderated to Rs. 82.3 crore from Rs. 131.9 crore on March 31, 2025, following their utilisation towards capex and working capital 
requirements. Consequently, the company moved from a net cash position of Rs. 40.1 crore as on March 31, 2025 to a net debt 
of Rs. 41.1 crore as on March 31, 2026. Nevertheless, APL’s dependence on borrowings remains limited.

Credit challenges 
 
Profitability indicators exposed to volatility in raw material prices– The company’s revenues and margins are exposed to 
fluctuations in the prices of key raw materials such as PVC/cPVC resins and HDPE granules. Any sharp movement in raw material 
prices could adversely impact the margins, considering the highly competitive industry and the time lag in passing on the higher 
costs to customers. Additionally, volatility in raw material prices can result in inventory gains or losses, which in turn impacts 
the profitability. This was evident in Q1 FY2027, when a sharp correction in PVC resin prices resulted in sizeable inventory losses, 
adversely impacting the operating profitability. Hence, the company’s ability to mitigate the price volatility will remain a key 
monitorable. 
Elevated competitive intensity in PVC pipe segment – The domestic pipes and fitting industry is characterised by the presence 
of large competitors as well as several mid-sized unorganised players. Given the elevated competitive intensity, the company’s 
pricing flexibility remains limited. 
Environment and social risks 
Environmental considerations – APL is exposed to risks arising from tightening environmental regulations, specifically pertaining 
to effluent discharge and treatment, waste management and operational safety. These may necessitate additional investments 
to comply with evolving regulatory standards. As per the company’s disclosures, APL assesses the environmental risks associated 
with its activities and has established procedures for identifying and managing such hazards. In FY2026, APL remained compliant 
with the applicable environmental laws and regulations, including the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, the Air 
(Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act and the Environment (Protection) Act, with no reported non-compliances or regulatory 
penalties. The established operating practices and compliance framework mitigate the environmental regulatory risks to an 
extent. 
 
Social considerations – The societal shift towards non-plastic products, given the environmental and health concerns associated 
with plastics, could structurally reduce the demand for APL’s products over the long term. However, such changes in consumer 
behaviour are expected to be gradual. Hence, given the gradual nature of this transition, the risk is not expected to materially 
affect APL’s credit profile over the medium term. 
 
Liquidity position: Adequate 
APL’s liquidity position remains adequate, supported by cash and liquid investments of Rs. 82.3 crore as on March 31, 2026, in 
addition to a sufficient headroom in the sanctioned working capital facilities. The working capital limits remained comfortably 
utilised below 50%, providing cushion in working capital limits. The liquidity is further supported by the balance proceeds of 
around Rs.82.5 crore from the Kitara Capital warrants received in October 2026, and the proposed fresh warrant issue of up to 
Rs. 189.1 crore which will support the near-term expansion plans to a large extent. 
Rating sensitivities 
Positive factors – ICRA could upgrade APL’s rating if the entity is able to demonstrate a sustained scale-up in its revenues and 
earnings while maintaining stable credit metrics and stronger return metrics. 
Negative factors – Pressure on APL’s rating could arise if the linkages between APL and the APL Apollo Group weaken, resulting 
in a moderation of its financial flexibility. A sustained moderation in the revenues and/or operating profits, a materially large 
debt-funded capex or a stretch in the working capital weakening the company’s credit metrics and/or liquidity position may also

About the company 
 
Apollo Pipes Limited (APL), incorporated in 2000, is a listed company belonging to the Sudesh Group and has its headquarters 
in Delhi NCR. APL has established a strong presence in the piping and allied product segment, marketing its products under the 
well-recognised APL Apollo brand. The company has an aggregate installed capacity of ~1,56,000 TPA as on March 31, 2024, 
spread across five facilities at Dadri and Sikanderabad (Uttar Pradesh), Ahmedabad (Gujarat), Raipur (Chhattisgarh) and 
Bengaluru (Karnataka), manufacturing a diverse range of products such as polyvinyl chloride (PVC) pipes, chlorinated polyvinyl 
chloride (cPVC) pipes, high density polyethylene (HDPE) pipes, bath fitting, solvent cement and water tanks. APL has also 
recently commissioned a plant at Varanasi with a capacity of 30,000 TPA.  
The company caters to various end-user segments such as water and sewerage transportation, irrigation, and plumbing 
applications in domestic and commercial construction. Its product profile includes over 1,500 varieties of cPVC, uPVC, and HDPE 
pipes, PVC taps and fittings that serve a wide range of applications across the agriculture, water management, construction, 
infrastructure and telecom ducting segments.  
APL’s extensive distribution network is spread across more than 600 channel partners. APL had acquired a 53.57% stake in Kisan 
Mouldings Ltd (KML) for Rs. 118.40 crore on March 26, 2024, aiming to bolster its position in the PVC pipes and fittings sector. 
KML has a total capacity of 60,000 MTPA. The proceeds of the equity infusion were utilised towards a one-time settlement 
(OTS) process with the lenders, thereby making the entity debt free.
