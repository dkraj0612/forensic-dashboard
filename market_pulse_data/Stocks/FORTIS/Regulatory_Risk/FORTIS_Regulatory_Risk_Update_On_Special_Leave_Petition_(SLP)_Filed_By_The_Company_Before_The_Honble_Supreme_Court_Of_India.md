Dear Sir/Madam, 
 
In furtherance of our earlier disclosures dated September 23, 2022, September 1, 2026 and September, 16, 2026, 
we would like to inform you that the Hon’ble Supreme Court, vide its order dated 25 September, 2026 (“Order”), 
has disposed of a Special Leave Petition (“SLP”) filed by the Company challenging the order dated August 31, 
2026 passed by the Hon’ble Delhi High Court (“Judgment”) in the matter of Daiichi Sankyo Company, Limited 
vs. Malvinder Mohan Singh & Ors. The SLP had inter alia challenged the direction for a forensic audit qua the 
Company contained in the Judgment, as well as certain observations made therein concerning the Company. 
 
By way of the Order, the Hon’ble Supreme Court has allowed the forensic audit to proceed, while expressly 
clarifying that various paragraphs in the Judgment, including certain specific paragraphs identified by the 
Hon’ble Court, containing observations challenged by the Company, are “tentative and only for the purpose of 
making out a case for forensic audit.” The Hon’ble Court has further clarified that “It goes without saying that 
the forensic audit shall be conducted independently, without being influenced by the abovementioned 
observations.” 
 
The Hon’ble Supreme Court has accordingly clarified that the various paragraphs of the Judgment shall not 
influence the conduct or outcome of the forensic audit, and that the forensic audit is required to be conducted 
independently of all such observations. Further, as stated in our previous disclosure dated September 1, 2026, 
the Hon’ble Delhi High Court has not imposed any liability, penalty or fine on the Company.  
 
It is extremely important to note that: 
 
(i) the Company is a complete stranger to the underlying dispute between Daiichi Sankyo and the Singh 
Brothers, and accordingly was never a party to the arbitration proceedings between them, and is neither a 
judgment debtor nor a garnishee in respect of Daiichi Sankyo’s decree; 
(ii) being a public listed company, the Company had no power or ability to control the transfer of shares by its 
erstwhile promoters, who were the owners of such shares, and the Company received no monies or proceeds 
on account of the dissipation of the erstwhile promoters’ shareholding;
