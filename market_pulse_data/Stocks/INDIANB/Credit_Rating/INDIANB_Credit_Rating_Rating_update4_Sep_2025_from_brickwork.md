RATING ACTION / OUTLOOK: REAFFIRMATION/ STABLE/ WITHDRAWAL 
Brickwork Ratings (BWR) withdraws the rating for the Basel III Tier-II bond (Series I) 
amounting to Rs. 500 Crores (present outstanding nil), on account of full redemption at its 
maturity, and reaffirms the ratings at BWR AAA/Stable for the Basel III Tier-II bond (Series II) 
issues amounting to Rs.1,000 Crores of Indian Bank. 
 
The rating reaffirmation continues to factor in the strong support of the Government of India, as 
the promoter and major shareholder with a 73.84% stake in Indian Bank as of 30 June 2025.  The 
rating factors in the established market position, comfortable capital adequacy, and resource 
profile and improved profitability. The rating also factors in the moderate, albeit improving, asset 
quality. BWR maintains a Stable outlook for the bank as the bank’s business risk profile is likely 
to remain unchanged over the medium term, indicating a low likelihood of a rating change over 
the medium term 
 
The Withdrawal of Rating of Tier II Bonds - Basel III (Series I) amounting to  Rs. 500 Crores 
(present outstanding nil), was on account of redemption in full upon its maturity in Jan 2025, as 
conveyed by the bank and confirmed by the DebentureTrustee. Withdrawal of Rating is in 
compliance with BWR Rating Withdrawal Policy.

KEY COVENANTS OF THE INSTRUMENT/FACILITY RATED:  
The Unsecured redeemable non-convertible Basel III compliant tier II bonds (Series II) of

Key Rating Drivers 
 
Credit Strengths-: 
●​ Strong support and majority ownership of the Government of India: The bank has 
high sovereign support, with the GOI holding 73.84% as of 30 June 2025. The GOI’s 
support of public sector banks (PSBs) has always been of prime importance. As the key 
promoter-shareholder, the GOI has continued to support PSBs, even in times of distress, 
through various recapitalization plans announced over the years. The GOI’s support to the 
bank in the form of capital infusion is expected to continue, given that the bank is one of 
the major PSBs in the country. The GOI is expected to provide capital support to the 
bank, as and when required, for business growth, as well as to strengthen the balance 
sheet to cover any asset-side risks.  
 
●​ Established track record and increase in the scale of operations: Indian Bank was 
established on 15 August 1907, has an established operational track record of around 
eleven decades, and is among the large PSBs in India in terms of business and outreach. 
The total business size has been improving and stood at Rs. 13,45,436 Cr as of 30 June 
2025 against Rs. 12,20,306 Cr. as of 30 June 2024, showing a Y-o-Y increase of 10.25% 
The bank also reported a marginal growth of 1.52% in total business during Q1FY26 as 
compared to FY25. As of 30 June 2025, the Global deposits (Domestic & Overseas) grew 
by 9.26% Y-o-Y to Rs 744,289 Cr from Rs 681,183 Cr as of 30 June 2024, which also 
marginally improved from Rs 737,154 Cr as at the end of FY25. The increase in deposits 
from FY25 to Q1FY26 was on account of an increase in the Term deposits by ~Rs.. 
12,873 Cr during Q1FY26, although the increase in deposits was partially offset by a 
decline in CASA deposits by ~Rs.. 5738 Cr during Q1FY26. The CASA deposits were Rs 
277,116 Cr as of 30 June 2025. The Global Advances stood at Rs 6,01,147 Cr as of 30 
June 2025, compared with Rs. 5,39,123 Cr as of 30 June 2024. Domestic deposits were 
95.36 % of Global deposits as of 30 June 2025 whereas domestic advances were 92.47% 
of total advances. The domestic advances book was split up into Retail, Agri, MSME, and 
Corporate loans, which accounted for 22.25%, 25.93%, 17.16% and 34.66% respectively 
of total domestic advances as of 30 June 2025. The share of RAM advances was 
maintained at 64% levels as at the end of FY25, which increased to 65.34% as of 30 June 
2025. The Bank has 5466 ATMs & Bunch Note Acceptors(BNAs) and 15080 Business 
Correspondents (BCs). The GoI’s stake continued to be 73.84% as of 30 June 2025. The 
bank also has three overseas branches located in Singapore, Colombo, and Jaffna. 
Business of Rs 57955 Cr has been generated through Digital Channels in Q1FY26.

●​ Comfortable capitalization: The Bank’s capital-to-risk-weighted assets ratio (CRAR) 
stood at 17.94% at the end of FY25 and was 16.44% as at the end of FY24. The bank 
maintained CRAR well above the minimum regulatory requirement of 11.5% (including 
capital conservation buffer of 2.5%). The Tier I Capital adequacy ratio improved to 
15.85% at the end of FY25 as compared to  14.03% at the end of FY24. The Common 
Equity Tier 1 (CET 1) ratio was 15.36% at the end of FY25, an improvement from  
13.52% at the end of FY24. As of 30 June 2025, the total CRAR of the bank was 17.80%, 
Tier I ratio was 15.74% and the Common Equity Tier 1 (CET 1) ratio was 15.26% as of 
30 June 2025.  
The bank’s total capital to risk weighted asset ratio, the Tier I ratio and CET-1 ratio 
continued to remain strong and well above the regulatory requirements of a minimum 
Common Equity Tier-1 (CET1) capital ratio of 8.0% (including the capital conservation 
buffer of 2.5%), and a minimum Tier 1 capital ratio of 9.5%, a minimum total capital 
adequacy ratio (total CRAR) of 11.5% as of 30 June 2025. 
 
●​ Improvement in Profitability: Net profit improved by 23.72% in Q1FY26 to Rs 2973 
Cr from Rs 2403 Cr in Q1FY25 due to an increase in total income by 10.48% to Rs 
18721 Cr supported 8.27% y-o-y increase in interest income to Rs 16283 Cr. Net interest 
income grew by 2.93% to Rs 6359 Cr in Q1FY26 as compared to Rs 6178 Cr in Q1FY25 
due to an 8.27% rise in interest income in Q1FY26 from Q1FY25. However, NIM 
declined marginally by 21 bps to 3.23% from 3.44% in Q1FY25 due to a 12% increase in 
interest expenses. Cost-to-income ratio increased to 45.78% in Q1FY26  by 147 bps from 
Q1FY25 owing to a 12.42% increase in operating expenses, which outpaced the growth 
of total income (10.48%). ROE and ROA both improved in Q1FY26 from 19.76% and 
1.2% respectively in Q1FY25 due to an improvement in net profit. Cost of deposit 
marginally increased y-o-y by 9 bps from 5.05% in Q1FY25, whereas Yield on Advance 
declined marginally by 11 bps to 8.58% in Q1FY26 as compared to 8.69% in Q1FY25.  
 
Net profit rose by 35.41% y-o-y to Rs. 10,918 Cr in FY25 from Rs. 8,063 Cr in FY24, 
primarily driven by healthy growth in total income (+12.20% y-o-y) and a sharp 
reduction in provisioning costs (-52.02% y-o-y), partly offset by higher operating 
expenses (+7.70% y-o-y). Net interest income (NII) registered a growth of 8.17%, 
reaching Rs. 25,176 Cr in FY25 as against Rs. 23,274 Cr in FY24. However, with the 
increase in cost of deposits to 5.12% and a corresponding 13.87% rise in interest 
expenses during FY25, the Net Interest Margin (NIM) moderated to 3.41% in FY25 from 
3.47% in FY24. The decline in NIM was partially cushioned by an improvement in yield 
on advances to 8.75% in FY25 (vs. 8.72% in FY24), despite the overall cost of funds 
rising to 5.21% in FY25. ROE and ROA improved in FY25 to 20.76% and 1.32%  
respectively, from 19.24% and 1.07% in FY24 due to improvement in net profit. 
 
Credit Risks-: 
●​ Improving but modest Asset Quality:  
The asset quality of the Bank continued to improve as Gross NPA (%) reduced by 76 bps 
on a YoY basis from 3.77% as of 30 June 2024 to 3.01% as of 30 June 2025 (on a q-o-q 
basis, GNPA improved from 3.09% as of 31 March 2025). The decline in the Retail and 
MSME sector GNPA in Q1FY26 contributed to the overall decline in NPA q-o-q, which,

sector. The improvement in GNPA was further supported by the reduction in slippages by 
4.24% q-o-q in Q1FY26 (Rs 1393 Cr in Q4FY25) and 30.81% y-o-y (Rs 1928 Cr in 
Q1FY25) to Rs 1334 Cr. Consequently, the slippage ratio declined to 0.94% in Q1FY26

Net NPA (%) improved by 21 bps on a YoY basis from 0.39% as of 30 June 2024 to

ratio excluding Technical Write Off (PCR) increased to 94.27 % as of 30 June 2025 from 
90.02% as of 30 June 2024 and 93.90% as of 31 March 2025, which indicates the Bank 
has sufficient provisions to withstand losses.  
 
The  Asset quality has improved over the years reflected by the Gross NPA and Net NPA 
ratio declining to 3.09% and 0.19% as of FY25 from 3.95% and 0.43% respectively as of 
FY24 (FY23:5.95% and 0.90% respectively) on account of write off and recoveries. This 
is also evidenced by the lower slippage ratio, which improved to 1.11% in FY25 from 
1.49% in FY24. This is further backed by an adequate PCR ratio, which strengthened to 
93.90% as of 31 March 2025 from 89.47% as of 31 March 2024.

RATING SENSITIVITIES 
 
Positive: Not applicable.  
Negative: A reduction in government support and the GoI’s stake in the bank falling below 51% 
could adversely impact the ratings and/or outlook. A substantial deterioration in Indian Bank’s 
asset quality, impacting significantly on the profitability and capitalization metrics, could be the 
key rating sensitivities 
 
LIQUIDITY POSITION - ADEQUATE 
The Bank’s overall liquidity profile remains adequate with no negative cumulative mismatches in 
the near to medium term as per their asset liability management statement as of  31 Mar 2025. 
The LCR of the bank as of 31 March 2025  was  126.62% and as of June 30 2025, was 137.44%. 
As of 30 June 2025, Cash and balance with RBI stood strong at Rs 33106 Cr as of 30 June 2025 
(increased from Rs 31948 Cr as of 31 March 2025) and balances with banks at Rs 20358 Cr 
(declined from Rs 22920 Cr as of 31 March 2025). As of 30 June 2025, cash and bank balances 
were Rs. 53464 Cr,, investments were Rs. 233,467 Cr, and the borrowings were Rs.  53007 Cr.

COMPANY PROFILE 
Indian Bank was established on August 15, 1907, as a part of the Swadeshi movement. The 
amalgamation of Allahabad Bank into Indian Bank has placed Indian Bank as the seventh-largest 
PSB with a total business of Rs 13.45 Lakh Cr as of 30 June 2025 as against Rs 13.25 lakh Cr as 
of 31 March 2025 (Rs 12.20 Lakh Cr as of 30 June 2024). As of 30 June 2025,  the Bank had 
5909 domestic branches, out of which 1,993 are Rural, 1,560 are Semi- Urban, 1,185 are Urban 
and 1,171 branches are in Metro Cities. The Bank has 5466 ATMs & Bunch Note 
Acceptors(BNAs) and 15080 number of Business Correspondents (BCs). The GoI’s stake 
continued to be 73.84% as of 30 June 2025. The bank also has three overseas branches located in 
Singapore, Colombo, and Jaffna. Business of Rs 57955 Cr has been generated through Digital 
Channels in Q1FY26.  
Shri Shanti Lal Jain, the previous Managing Director & Chief Executive Officer of Indian Bank, 
retired as of 31 Dec 2024. Mr Binod Kumar has been appointed as the new Managing Director 
and CEO of Indian Bank on 16 Jan 2025 for a period of three years. He was the executive 
director of Punjab National Bank. Shri Mahesh Kumar Bajaj, Executive Director of the Bank, 
demitted office on 30.06.2025 on superannuation. The bank had total (domestic & overseas) 
deposits of Rs. 7.44 lakh Cr and total (domestic & overseas) advances of Rs. 6.01 lakh Cr as of 
30 June 2025. CASA ratio (domestic) was 38.97% in Q1FY26, reduced from 40.17% in 
Q4FY25.
