Rationale and key rating drivers 
CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) has placed long-term bank facilities of Automobile Corporation of Goa Limited (ACGL) 
on Rating Watch with Positive Implications while reaffirming the short term ratings, following the expected increase in Tata Motors 
Limited’s  (TML’s, CARE AA+/Stable/CARE A1+) ownership in ACGL, arising from the recently completed share buyback. ACGL’s 
Board had approved the buyback of up to 1,44,639 equity shares, representing 2.38% of its outstanding equity, for a maximum 
consideration of ~₹29 crore. The company has since announced the closure/completion of the buyback process. As TML, which 
held 48.98% in ACGL, did not participate in the buyback, its shareholding is expected to increase to ~50.17% upon extinguishment 
of bought-back shares. The resultant change in shareholding structure is expected to lead to ACGL transitioning from an associate 
company to a subsidiary of TML, strengthening the ownership linkage between these two entities. CareEdge Ratings will continue 
to monitor the completion of share extinguishment process and revised shareholding pattern and assess potential credit-positive 
implications, arising from the strengthened parent-subsidiary relationship. 
 
Ratings continue to derive strength from ACGL’s improving operating and financial performance, supported by healthy growth in 
volumes, better realisations, and improved profitability. Ratings also factor in ACGL’s strong operational and managerial linkages 
with TML and its strategic position as a key supplier, meeting around one-third of TML’s bus-body requirements, resulting in a 
significant share of ACGL’s revenue being derived from TML. ACGL has also increased its installed bus-manufacturing capacity to 
12,000 units from 10,000 units in the previous year. However, ratings remain constrained by delays in timely pass-through of 
changes in input costs, cyclical nature of the commercial vehicle industry, and competition from global players in the bus segment.

Detailed description of key rating drivers 
 
Key strengths 
Strong linkages with Tata Motors Limited (TML) 
ACGL maintains strong operational linkages with TML, serving as one of its key suppliers for bus body requirements and catering 
to 1/3rd of TML’s internal combustion engine (ICE) bus body needs. Girish Wagh, Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer 
(MD and CEO) of TML, also serves as a Non-Executive, Non-Independent Director on the Board of ACGL, and Venkata Gopal 
Ramanan, Chief Finance Officer (CFO) of TML since October 1, 2025, also serves as a Non-Executive, Non-Independent Director 
of ACGL. The company derives a substantial portion of its revenue from TML, which accounted for ~88% of total revenue in FY26. 
In line with these strong linkages, analytical approach for rating TML factors in the consolidation of ACGL.

Healthy operational performance 
The company reported healthy growth in its total operating income (TOI), which increased by 41.5% to ₹937.38 crore in FY26 
(PY: ₹662.55 crore), supported by better realisations and higher volumes. Bus sales touched an all-time high of 9,328 units (PY: 
7,265 units), lifting capacity utilisation to 77.7% (PY: 73%). Profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation, and taxation 
(PBILDT) margins improved to 9.54% in FY26 (PY: 8.17%). In Q2FY27, total income stood at ₹265.59 crore (Q2FY26: ₹206.38 
crore), with PBILDT of ₹14.31 crore (Q2FY26: ₹17.04 crore) and a margin of 5.39% (Q2FY26: 8.26%). Margins moderated during 
the period, primarily due to an unfavourable product mix and lower international business (IB) volumes. Margins are expected to 
improve with easing of geopolitical uncertainties and recovery in IB volumes; however, the extent and timing of improvement 
remain monitorable. 
 
Robust financial risk profile 
The company is net debt-free and has no long-term debt obligations. Total debt of ₹9.52 crore in FY26 (₹85.74 crore in FY25), 
primarily represents outstanding customer bill discounting facilities, which are availed in the company’s name but ultimately 
settled by TML. Since these facilities are on a recourse basis, they are classified as short-term borrowings in financials until 
settlement by TML. Overall gearing improved to 0.03x as on March 31, 2026 (PY: 0.34x), driven by an 88.9% reduction in total 
debt to ₹9.52 crore (PY: ₹85.74 crore), following lower year-end bill-discounting utilisation. It is expected to remain in a similar 
range going forward. In FY26, the company declared a dividend of ₹16.74 crore, representing ~24% of its profit after tax. 
CareEdge Ratings expects that the company’s capital structure will remain strong in the near-to-medium term, in the absence of 
major debt-funded capex plans. 
 
Liquidity: Strong 
The company maintains a strong liquidity position, with cash and bank balances and liquid investments amounting to ~₹170 crore 
in Q2FY27. Fund-based utilisation stood at ~26%, while non-fund-based utilisation stood at ~29%, providing additional liquidity 
headroom. In FY26, the company generated gross cash accruals of ₹75.73 crore. ACGL has planned a capex of ~₹10-15 crore in 
FY27, primarily for capacity expansion and maintenance. The entire capex will be funded through internal accruals and cash and 
bank balance, with no reliance on additional debt. 
 
Key weaknesses 
Cyclicality in the commercial vehicle (CV) industry  
The company’s revenue remains closely linked to bus segment of the commercial vehicle (CV) industry, which is inherently cyclical 
and influenced by government budget allocations for public transportation and broader economic conditions. Demand volatility in 
this segment often results in underutilised capacity, higher fixed cost absorption, and delays in achieving operational efficiencies, 
exerting pressure on cash flows.  
 
Intense competition  
The bus body-building industry is witnessing increasing competitive intensity with entry of global players and expansion of 
domestic manufacturers. This trend could put pressure on established original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), such as TML, 
which in turn may impact ACGL’s performance in the future, despite the company’s current strong growth trajectory. ACGL’s 
revenue concentration remains high, with ~88% of its FY26 turnover derived from TML. While this dependence on a single 
customer limits diversification and heightens exposure to industry-wide risks, it also provides revenue stability and long-term 
growth visibility, given TML’s strong market position and ACGL’s role as a key strategic supplier.

Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks  
Particulars 
Risk Factors 
Environmental 
ACGL commissioned solar plants of 500 kWp in Goa and 805 kWp at Jejuri in FY26. Together with energy-
efficiency initiatives, these reduced carbon emissions by over 600 tonnes during the year. The company 
also converted a diesel-operated painting oven to electricity, commissioned 130 KLD of sewage-treatment 
capacity and food-waste composting capacity of 550 kg per day, and diverted hazardous, Fiber-Reinforced 
Polymer (FRP), and glass waste for authorised processing or recycling. An 85-lakh-litre rainwater-harvesting 
lake with six groundwater-recharge wells remains under development in Goa.  
Social 
ACGL conducted regular safety, ergonomics, fire-fighting and emergency-response programmes for 
employees and contract workers. Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) expenditure increased by 59.8% to 
₹94.57 lakh in FY26 (PY: ₹59.18 lakh), mainly towards community healthcare, vocational training, inclusive 
education, and school infrastructure. No sexual-harassment complaints were received or carried forward. 
Governance 
As on March 31, 2026, ACGL’s nine-member Board comprised four independent directors, including three 
women, four non-independent non-executive directors, and one executive director and CEO.

ACGL was incorporated in 1980 and was jointly promoted by the Economic Development Corporation (EDC) of Goa Limited, a 
Government of Goa undertaking, TML, and Tata Motor Finance Limited (TMFL, now merged to Tata Capital Limited: CARE AAA; 
Stable), a Tata Group entity. TML holds a 48.98% direct stake in the company, while TCL holds 0.79%, and EDC holds 6.66%. 
The company operates through two business segments: the pressing division and the bus body building division. The pressing 
division is engaged in manufacturing pressed parts, components, sub-assemblies, and assemblies, while the bus body building 
division focuses on manufacturing bus bodies and component parts. The product portfolio includes staff transport buses, luxury 
buses, city transport buses, defence buses, airport buses, school buses, sleeper coaches, special application vehicles, and sheet 
metal products.
