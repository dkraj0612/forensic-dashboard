Rationale and key rating drivers 
Reaffirmation of ratings assigned to bank facilities of Automobile Corporation of Goa Limited (ACGL) reflects consistent 
improvements in its operating and financial performance, supported by healthy growth in volumes, improved realisations and 
profitability. Ratings also factor in strong operational and managerial linkages with Tata Motors Limited (TML; rated CARE AA+; 
Stable/CARE A1+), which holds 48.98% stake in ACGL as on September 03, 2026. ACGL remains a strategic supplier to TML, 
catering to 1/3rd of its bus body requirements, leading to a significant portion of ACGL’s revenue being sourced from TML. The 
company has enhanced its installed bus-manufacturing capacity to 12,000 (PY:10,000). However, ratings remain constrained by 
delays in the timely pass-through of input-cost changes, cyclicality in commercial vehicles industry, and competition from global 
players in the bus segment. TML’s demerger will have no impact on ACGL’s operations, as the company’s business will continue 
seamlessly under TML’s commercial vehicle business segment post-demerger.

Analytical approach:  
Factoring strong operational and managerial linkages with TML. For arriving at ratings, CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) 
has used its notch-up framework, considering the support derived from being part of TML. 
 
Outlook: Stable 
CareEdge Ratings has assigned a Stable outlook to ACGL’s facilities, supported by its strong operational and managerial linkages 
with TML, sustained business growth, and a healthy financial profile. The company’s scale is expected to further improve, driven 
by higher bus demand from TML.

Healthy operational performance 
The company reported healthy growth in its total operating income (TOI), which increased by 41.5% to ₹937.38 crore in FY26 
(PY: ₹662.55 crore), supported by better realisations and higher volumes. Bus sales touched an all-time high of 9,328 units (PY: 
7,265 units), lifting capacity utilisation to 77.7% (PY: 73%). PBILDT margins improved to 9.54% in FY26 (PY: 8.17%). In Q1FY27, 
total income stood at ₹248.94 crore (Q1FY26: ₹256.41 crore), with PBILDT of ₹13.15 crore (Q1FY26: ~₹20.95 crore) and a 
margin of 5.28% (PY: 10.11%). Margins moderated during the period primarily due to an unfavourable product mix and lower 
International business (IB) volumes. Margins are expected to improve with the easing of geopolitical uncertainties and recovery 
in IB volumes; however, the extent and timing of improvement remain monitorable. 
 
Robust financial risk profile 
The company is net debt-free and has no long-term debt obligations. Total debt of ₹9.52 crore in FY26 (₹85.74 crore in FY25), 
primarily represents outstanding customer bill discounting facilities, which are availed in the company’s name but ultimately 
settled by TML. Since these facilities are on a recourse basis, they are classified as short-term borrowings in the financials until 
settlement by TML. Overall gearing improved to 0.03x as on March 31, 2026 (PY: 0.34x), driven by an 88.9% reduction in total 
debt to ₹9.52 crore (PY: ₹85.74 crore), following lower year-end bill-discounting utilisation. It is expected to remain in a similar 
range going forward. During the year, the company declared a dividend of ₹16.74 crore, representing ~24% of its profit after 
tax. CareEdge Ratings expects that the company’s capital structure will remain strong in the near-to-medium term in the absence 
of major debt-funded capex plans. 
 
Liquidity: Strong 
The company maintains a strong liquidity position, with cash and bank balances and liquid investments amounting to ~₹200 crore 
in Q1FY27. Fund-based utilisation stood at ~26%, while non-fund-based utilisation stood at ~29%, providing additional liquidity 
headroom. In FY26, the company generated gross cash accruals of ₹75.73 crore. ACGL has planned a capex of ~₹10-15 crore 
for the year, primarily for capacity expansion and maintenance. The entire capex will be funded through internal accruals and 
cash and bank balance, with no reliance on additional debt. 
 
Key weaknesses 
 
Cyclicality in the Commercial Vehicle (CV) Industry  
The company’s revenue remains closely linked to the bus segment of the commercial vehicle (CV) industry, which is inherently 
cyclical and influenced by government budget allocations for public transportation as well as broader economic conditions. 
Demand volatility in this segment often results in underutilized capacity, higher fixed cost absorption, and delays in achieving 
operational efficiencies, thereby exerting pressure on cash flows.  
 
Intense competition  
The bus body-building industry is witnessing increasing competitive intensity with the entry of global players and the expansion 
of domestic manufacturers. This trend could put pressure on established OEMs such as TML, which in turn may impact ACGL’s 
performance in the future, despite the company’s current strong growth trajectory. Moreover, ACGL’s revenue concentration 
remains high, with ~88% of its FY26 turnover derived from TML. While this dependence on a single customer limits diversification 
and heightens exposure to industry-wide risks, it also provides revenue stability and long-term growth visibility, given TML’s 
strong market position and ACGL’s role as a key strategic supplier.

Environmental 
ACGL commissioned solar plants of 500 kWp in Goa and 805 kWp at Jejuri in FY26. Together with energy-
efficiency initiatives, these reduced carbon emissions by over 600 tonnes during the year. The company 
also converted a diesel-operated painting oven to electricity, commissioned 130 KLD of sewage-treatment 
capacity and food-waste composting capacity of 550 kg per day, and diverted hazardous, Fiber-Reinforced 
Polymer (FRP) and glass waste for authorised processing or recycling. An 85-lakh-litre rainwater-harvesting 
lake with six groundwater-recharge wells remains under development in Goa.  
Social 
ACGL conducted regular safety, ergonomics, fire-fighting and emergency-response programmes for 
employees and contract workers. Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) expenditure increased by 59.8% to 
₹94.57 lakh in FY26 (PY: ₹59.18 lakh), mainly towards community healthcare, vocational training, inclusive 
education and school infrastructure. No sexual-harassment complaints were received or carried forward. 
Governance 
As on March 31, 2026, ACGL’s nine-member Board comprised four independent directors, including three 
women, four non-independent non-executive directors, and one Executive Director and CEO.

ACGL was incorporated in 1980 and was jointly promoted by the Economic Development Corporation of Goa Limited (EDC), a 
Government of Goa undertaking, TML, and Tata Motor Finance Limited (TMFL, now merged to Tata Capital Limited: CARE AAA; 
Stable), a Tata Group entity. TML holds a 48.98% direct stake in the company, while TCL holds 0.79%, and EDC holds 6.66%. 
The company operates through two business segments: the Pressing Division and the Bus Body Building Division. The Pressing 
Division is engaged in manufacturing pressed parts, components, sub-assemblies, and assemblies, while the Bus Body Building 
Division focuses on manufacturing bus bodies and component parts. The product portfolio includes staff transport buses, luxury 
buses, city transport buses, defence buses, airport buses, school buses, sleeper coaches, special application vehicles, and sheet 
metal products.
