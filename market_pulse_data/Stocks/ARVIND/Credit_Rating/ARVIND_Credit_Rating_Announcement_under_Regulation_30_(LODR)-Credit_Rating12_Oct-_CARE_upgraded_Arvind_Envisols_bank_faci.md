Rationale and key rating drivers 
Upgrade in ratings assigned to bank facilities of Arvind Envisol Limited (AEL) factors in improvement in its scale of operations 
marked by total operating income (TOI) and profitability in FY26, considering healthy order execution, improved operating 
efficiencies and benefits of operating leverage. A strong outstanding order book as on August 31, 2026, also provides medium-
term revenue visibility. Ratings also consider strong financial risk profile of its parent, Arvind Limited (Arvind; CARE AA; Stable/ 
CARE A1+; LT rating upgraded from CARE AA-; Stable and ST rating reaffirmed in August 2026) and benefits derived from its 
strong parentage. Ratings continue to factor AEL’s established execution track record, proprietary patented technology, reputed 
clientele, comfortable capital structure, strong debt coverage indicators and adequate liquidity. 
 
These rating strengths are partially offset by AEL’s working capital intensive operations, concentration of order-book to a few 
large orders, profitability susceptible to input prices and inherent engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) execution 
risks. Competition in the fragmented wastewater-treatment industry remains an additional constraint. 
 
Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions 
Positive factors 
• 
Growth in the scale of operations to over ₹500 crore with ROCE of over 25% on a sustained basis. 
• 
Significant improvement in Arvind’s credit profile. 
 
Negative factors  
• 
Deterioration in its liquidity profile and elongation of gross operating cycle beyond 150 days. 
• 
Deterioration in capital structure with total outside liability to total net worth (TOL/TNW) above 1.5x. 
• 
Change in stance of support from or major deterioration in Arvind’s credit profile. 
 
Analytical approach: Standalone and factoring the parentage of Arvind, which holds 100% shareholding in AEL. CARE Ratings 
Limited (CareEdge Ratings) has also applied parent notch-up framework to arrive at AEL’s rating.  
 
Outlook: Stable 
CareEdge Ratings believes AEL will continue to benefit from established relationships with clients leading to growth in revenue 
with steady profitability. 
 
Detailed description of key rating drivers 
 
Key strengths 
Improving scale of operations and profitability, strong order-book providing near-medium term revenue visibility 
AEL’s TOI grew by ~14% to ₹307 crore in FY26 (FY25: ₹268 crore), considering strong order execution. Order-inflow remained 
healthy amid rising industrialisation, growing water scarcity, stringent environmental regulations and greater emphasis on 
wastewater recycling and reuse. The company had an outstanding order book of ~₹419 crore as on August 31, 2026, providing 
revenue visibility in the medium term. The continued to receive order inflow and annual O&M contracts from reputed customers, 
which shall support its TOI to ~₹350-450 crore p.a. over the medium term.

Increase in scale, and improved operating efficiencies, resulted in a substantial improvement in PBILDT margin to 12.86% in FY26 
(FY25: 5.53%). Growth momentum continued in Q1FY27, with TOI increasing by ~35% y-o-y to ₹90 crore. PBILDT margin also 
improved to 7.96% in Q1FY27 (Q1FY26: 2.29%). Supported by the healthy order book and expected operating leverage, PBILDT 
margin is projected to remain healthy over the medium term. 
 
Strong parentage of Arvind  
Arvind is the flagship company of the Ahmedabad based Lalbhai group and one of India’s leading vertically integrated textile and 
technical textile companies with presence of over nine decades in the industry. Arvind is among the largest denim and woven 
fabric manufacturers, with an installed capacity of 60 million metres per annum (MMPA) and 180 MMPA, respectively, as on March 
31, 2026. Arvind manufactures a range of fabrics, garments and technical textiles/advanced material products.  
 
Through its wholly owned subsidiary AEL, Arvind is engaged in assembling and installing waste-water treatment plants. AEL is 
expected to benefit from strong parentage in future.  
 
In FY26, Arvind’s TOI grew by ~12% to ₹9,335 crore in FY26 (FY25: ₹8,351 crore) with PBILDT margin at 11.51%. Healthy net 
worth base of ₹4,113 crore as on March 31, 2026, supported a comfortable capital structure with overall gearing of 0.54x (PY: 
0.54x). 
 
In-house developed patented technology for waste-water treatment 
AEL has an established track record of ~15 years in the wastewater treatment industry and has executed over 750 projects across 
effluent treatment plants (ETPs), common effluent treatment plants (CETPs), sewage treatment plants (STPs) and zero liquid 
discharge (ZLD) systems. The company provides integrated solutions across the project lifecycle, encompassing plant designing, 
engineering, procurement and construction (EPC), operation and maintenance (O&M), and supply of components. 
 
AEL’s technological capabilities are supported by three patents registered across globe, including its proprietary Polymeric Film 
Evaporation Technology (PFET) for mechanical vapour recompression evaporators. PFET uses polymeric-film heat exchangers in 
place of conventional metallic heat exchangers and entails significantly lower energy consumption compared to steam-based 
evaporation systems, reducing the operating cost of evaporators. The company’s proprietary technology, and its established 
execution track record and end-to-end service capabilities, supports its competitive position in the wastewater treatment industry. 
 
Reputed clientele 
AEL has executed projects across diverse end-user industries, including textiles, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, steel, renewable 
energy and other industrial segments. Its customer base comprises reputed corporates and common effluent treatment 
companies. The presence of established customers across diverse industries supports repeat business prospects and mitigates 
counterparty and sector-concentration risks to an extent. 
 
Top 10 customers contributed ~67% of AEL’s revenue in FY26 (FY25: 64%), indicating moderate customer concentration. With 
a revenue contribution from Arvind of ~30% in FY26 (FY25: 33%). The established credit profile of key customers and 
diversification across industries partially mitigate the counterparty risk. 
 
Comfortable capital structure and debt coverage indicators 
AEL’s capital structure remained comfortable, with TOL/TNW improving to 0.81x as on March 31, 2026 (FY25: 0.95x), supported 
by accretion to reserves following healthy growth in profitability. Overall gearing remained comfortable at 0.21x as on March 31, 
2026 (FY25: 0.15x). With healthy operating profitability and moderate debt levels, total debt/PBILDT improved to 0.64x in FY26 
(FY25: 0.94x). Return on capital employed (ROCE) improved to 31.73% in FY26 and is projected to remain healthy in the medium 
term. In the absence of large debt-funded capex, capital structure and debt coverage indicators are expected to remain 
comfortable. 
 
Key weaknesses 
Large working capital requirement 
AEL’s operations are working capital intensive. Payment for AEL’s project orders is generally linked to project progress, and clients 
also retain a certain percentage of contract value as retention money which is paid only after quality of the work is established 
post completion of the contract. Its operations are reliant on non-fund-based working capital limits, which it utilises for providing 
financial bank guarantee (BG) against customer advances or mobilisation advances, performance BG to its clients and LC for 
procurement of raw material and component. The company’s gross operating cycle stood at 103 days in FY26 (FY25: 116 days) 
backed by collection period of 46 days and inventory holding period of 58 days.

Profitability susceptible to adverse movement in input prices and foreign exchange rates  
AEL’s profitability remains exposed to raw material price volatility, considering the fixed-price contracts and limited ability to pass 
on an increase in input costs. It is also susceptible to foreign-exchange fluctuations on the unhedged portion of imported 
membranes and overseas project receivables, although forex losses remained moderate at ₹0.72 crore in FY26 (FY25: ₹1.16 
crore). 
 
Presence in a fragmented and competitive construction industry 
Initiatives undertaken by the Government of India (GOI) to boost infrastructure has gradually resulted in an increased order 
inflow for the industry players. While the water treatment industry remains highly fragmented and competitive, AEL, as a mid-
sized but established player with patented technology and proven project execution capabilities, is positioned to benefit. Its 
domestic and international presence provides a competitive edge, though it continues to face pressure from larger established 
players and the unorganised sector. 
 
Liquidity: Adequate 
Despite working capital intensive operations, AEL’s liquidity remains adequate marked by cash and bank balance of ₹41 crore as 
on March 31, 2026, low utilisation of working capital limits (~9% for 12-months ended July 2026) and healthy current ratio of 
1.86x as on March 31, 2026, (PY: 1.71x). The company largely funds its working capital requirement through internal accruals 
and customer advances with lower reliance on external debt. AEL receives advance from the customer (non-interest bearing) to 
the extent of 10-15% of the order value, which supports AEL’s working capital requirement. AEL submits performance BG (PBG) 
to its customers to prevent blocking of funds in customer retention money. In the absence of term debt repayments obligation 
other than lease repayments and capex, the company’s liquidity is expected to remain adequate.
