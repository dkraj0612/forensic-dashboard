Rating Action: Moody's Ratings affirms Bank of Baroda's Baa3 ratings,
upgrades Baseline Credit Assessment
19 Nov 2026 
Singapore, November 19, 2026 -- Moody's Ratings (Moody's) has today affirmed Bank of Baroda's (BOB)
Baa3/P-3 long-term (LT) and short-term (ST) local-currency (LC) and foreign-currency (FC) deposit ratings,
the Baa3/P-3 LT and ST LC and FC Counterparty Risk Ratings (CRRs) as well as the Baa3(cr)/P-3(cr) LT
and ST Counterparty Risk (CR) Assessments.
We have also affirmed Bank of Baroda, London Branch's (P)Baa3 FC senior unsecured medium-term note
(MTN) program rating, Baa3/P-3 LT and ST FC and LC CRRs, as well as Baa3(cr)/P-3(cr) LT and ST CR
Assessments.
At the same time, we have upgraded BOB's Baseline Credit Assessment (BCA) and adjusted BCA to baa3
from ba1.
The rating outlook for BOB, where applicable, remains stable.
RATINGS RATIONALE
The affirmation of BOB's Baa3 ratings and upgrade of BCA to baa3 from ba1 reflects its improved
fundamentals, underpinned by strong solvency, funding and liquidity. It also reflects our expectation that its
credit profile will remain strong over the next 12 to 18 months, despite potential asset quality risk to the
bank's agriculture portfolio driven by environmental impact due to El Nino and the impact on capital due to
the implementation of the Expected Credit Loss (ECL) framework from 01 April 2027.
BOB's asset quality has improved significantly over the past five years. As of the end of September 2026,
gross nonperforming loan (NPL) ratio was at 1.8%, lower than 2.2% a year earlier, supported by lower
slippages and higher recoveries. However, we expect some asset quality pressure to emerge in the
agricultural portfolio, which accounted for over 13% of loans as of the same date, following the weaker
monsoon in the second half of 2026. We also expect micro, small and medium enterprises (MSME)
borrowers to be vulnerable amid high inflation due to their lower bargaining power to pass on higher costs.
We expect the bank to remain well capitalized, with a consolidated CET1 ratio of 14.7% as of the end of
September 2026. While the transition to ECL framework will reduce capital, the five-year transition period,
as well as capital release from risk weight changes to some exposures will limit the immediate pressure on
capitalization. Nevertheless, the transition to ECL will strengthen the bank's loss absorbing buffers.
Meanwhile, we expect return on tangible assets to moderate to about 1% over the next 12 to 18 months as
credit costs will increase due to the implementation of the ECL framework. However, the increase in policy
rates will stabilize net interest margins and help to offset the impact of higher credit costs.
Funding and liquidity will remain as key credit strengths, underpinned by the bank's strong deposit franchise
and a granular deposit base. As of the end of September 2026, BOB's current account and savings account
deposit ratio was at 36.4% and the bank has maintained its deposit market share at about 6% in recent
years.
At the same time, we expect the bank to continue to hold sufficient liquidity to meet short term liquidity

gaps. As of the end of September 2026, the bank's quarterly average liquidity coverage ratio was 133.6%
on a standalone basis.
While we expect the bank to receive support from the Government of India (Baa3 stable) in times of need,
its ratings do not benefit from any uplift because its BCA is already at the same level as India's sovereign
rating. Our support assumption takes into consideration the bank's importance to the domestic banking
sector and its affiliation with the government, which has a 63.97% stake in the bank as of the end of
September 2026.
FACTORS THAT COULD LEAD TO AN UPGRADE OR DOWNGRADE OF THE RATINGS
An upgrade to BOB's deposit ratings and BCA is unlikely because they are at the same level as India's
sovereign rating.
We would downgrade BOB's ratings if India's sovereign rating is downgraded, reflecting the strong linkages
between the bank's credit profile and that of the government. The deposit ratings could also be downgraded
if there is a multi-notch downgrade of the BCA.
The BCA could be downgraded if the bank's loan growth accelerates materially above system loan growth,
posing risks to asset quality, or if the bank's tangible common equity / risk-weighted assets (TCE/RWA)
ratio declines to below 11% or its net income/tangible assets ratio declines to below 0.5% on a sustained
basis.
PRINCIPAL METHODOLOGY
The principal methodology used in these ratings was Banks published in September 2026 and available at
https://ratings.moodys.com/rmc-documents/474049. Alternatively, please see the Rating Methodologies
page on https://ratings.moodys.com for a copy of this methodology.
The net effect of any adjustments applied to rating factor scores or scorecard outputs under the primary
methodology(ies), if any, was not material to the ratings addressed in this announcement.
Bank of Baroda is headquartered in Mumbai and reported consolidated assets of INR22.3 trillion as of 30
September 2026.
REGULATORY DISCLOSURES

For provisional ratings, the Credit Rating Announcement provides certain regulatory disclosures in relation
