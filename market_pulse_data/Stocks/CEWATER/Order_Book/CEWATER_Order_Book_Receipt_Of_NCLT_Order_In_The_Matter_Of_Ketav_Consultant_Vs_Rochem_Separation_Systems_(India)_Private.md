Applicant Creditor had relied upon these email communications as acknowledgement 
of debt for extension of limitation period and admission of Corporate Debtor in relation 
to such debt as being undisputed.  Admittedly, the said emails required the Operational 
Creditor to share a reconciliation, and those emails can only constitute admissions for 
an amount, if any, payable to the Operational Creditor in those emails. 
10.  It is noted that the Corporate Debtor had contemporaneously disputed and denied the 
amount, Rs.1,75,61,525/- as claimed by the Operational Creditor in its previous notice 
through reply dated August 14, 2023, and the said dispute was also communicated to 
NeSL in course of their authentication exercise.  It is also noted that the Reply to the 
notice of demand comprehensively lists the dispute in relation to the amount claimed 
and notes the previous communication between the parties in relation thereto.  These 
facts clearly demonstrate that the Corporate Debtor has been disputing the amount 
claimed in the Application as due and payable even prior to the issuance of the demand 
notice, and that there has been a pre-existing dispute in relation thereto.  
11. In the case of Salil Musale v. Lintec India Pvt. Ltd. and Ors., (2026) ibclaw.in 704 
NCLAT, it is held that “an application under Section 9 of IBC is not maintainable 
where notice of dispute was issued before and in reply to demand notice and the 
information utility record showed the debt as disputed”.  
12. In the present case also, the notice of dispute was issued before and in reply to demand 
notice and the information utility record showed the debt as disputed.  In view of the 
this, RCP(IB)/24(MB) of 2025 is Dismissed and disposed of.
