Foreign institutional investors’ holdings in Indian equities have grown 3.6 
times since 2012 to around $783 billion, even as their share of the market has 
fallen to a 17-year low of 15.1%. The decline in foreign ownership share 
reflects the faster growth of domestic participation, including retail investors, 
rather than simply a reduction in foreign holdings. Domestic equity 
participation in India remains below developed-market levels, suggesting 
there could be room for further growth in domestic flows.
❑
During H1:2026-27, money market rates softened and remained mostly range 
bound amid large surplus liquidity conditioned by robust capital inflows, 
especially from FCNR(B) deposits under the Reserve Bank’s swap facility 
announced in June 2026, which attracted $143 billion. However, owing to rise 
in inflation and deteriorating global environment, central bank has also 
tightened liquidity through OMO (open market operations) sales of G-secs, 
VRRR auctions and stricter daily CRR maintenance, following its recent shift to 
a tightening stance and the start of a rate-hike cycle by increasing repo rate 
from 5.25% to 5.5% in October MPC. 
❑
On a year-on-year (y-o-y) basis, non-food bank credit grew by 18.8% as on the 
fortnight ended August 31, 2026, compared to 10.2% during the 
corresponding fortnight of the previous year (September 5, 2025). 
❑
Credit to agriculture and allied activities registered a y-o-y growth of 17.2% 
vis-a-vis 7.6% in the corresponding fortnight of the previous year. Credit to 
services sector registered a y-o-y growth of 24.3% (10.3% in the 
corresponding fortnight of the previous year). 
❑
Credit to personal loans segment recorded a y-o-y growth of 16.9%, as 
compared with 11.9% a year ago. Segments such as ‘housing’ and ‘vehicle 
loans’ sustained double-digit growth, indicating steady demand. Growth then 
slowed quickly, from about 121% in April 2026 to 83.2% in August 2026, 
partly because of base effects and a possible cooling in gold prices or tighter 
regulatory norms on gold lending.

Environment
❑
The Bank has become a signatory of Partnership for Carbon Accounting Financials 
(PCAF). PCAF is a global non-profit organization that enables Banks to assess and 
disclose greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions (under Category 15 of Scope 3) 
associated with loans and investments. 
❑
The Bank has financed numerous projects pertaining to renewable energy. As on 
30th September 2026, corporate financing is ₹ 2392.57 crores towards 
renewable energy. 
❑
In MSME segment, Bank has financed ₹20.17 crores towards green financing. 
Further, the Bank has MSE GIFT Scheme to promote adoption of sustainable and 
eco-friendly practices, technologies and renewable energy sources in MSME 
enterprises sector. The Bank also has a scheme for the promotion of circular 
economy MSME SPICE incentivising MSEs to adopt circular economy. Bank has 
sanctioned ₹ 1.51 crores and ₹ 0.09 crores in the two schemes respectively as on 
30th September 2026. 
❑
Bank has recently launched Cent Green Home scheme on 31.08.2026 wherein 
first housing loan of ₹ 22.5 crore has been sanctioned. Green certification is 
mandatory for eligibility/availing benefits under the Scheme. Under PM Surya 
Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana sanctioned amount is ₹502.04 crores.
❑
Adoption of rooftop solar across owned premises, replacement of DG sets with 
inverters and usage of environment friendly gases in air conditioners are some of 
the operational level initiatives, Bank is adopting to reduce its Scope 1, Scope 2 
emissions. 
❑
Under PM Kusum and Compressed Biogas Scheme, the Bank has financed 
₹363.18 crores and ₹ 36.44 crores respectively as on 30th September 2026.
❑
Bank has a special term deposit scheme named ‘Cent Green Deposit’ with 
periods of deposit being 1111, 2222, 3333 days. As on 30th September 2026, the 
Bank has mobilized ₹ 463.76 crores under the same.

The Bank has a number of schemes 
regarding ‘reward and recognition’ for the

them. The Bank conducts regular awareness 
programs on the same to disseminate 
information.
