Mr. Saurabh 
Sangla: 
 
Shaft length is a consideration, but it does not by itself 
determine whether a shaft is suitable for defence applications. 
Torque capacity and the intended application are important. We 
already manufacture certain parts for tankers and have 
contracts with a couple of defence companies, including for 
truck shafts. 
Based on our current capabilities, we can manufacture shafts 
up to three metres long. A three-metre shaft is not generally 
used in military applications. Longer vehicles may use multiple 
shaft sections because the chassis moves during operation. 
Depending on the application, a shaft may have two, three or 
four sections. Our current capability extends to torque of up to 
47,000 newton-metres.

Mr. Saurabh 
Sangla:  
We have one of the world’s broadest ranges of propeller-shaft 
products, with more than 5,250 different part numbers. A 
customer in the US may require hundreds of different part 
numbers on a single shipment. We therefore need to hold 
inventory for some products, while other products may be in 
transit by sea for extended periods. These factors increase the 
working capital cycle. 
A broad range of SKUs also supports profitability because 
some products are manufactured by only a few companies 
globally. Certain products may sell only around 200 units a year 
but can deliver margins of approximately 50%. Inventory is held 
at different stages of our integrated process, including steel, 
forging, heat treatment, machining, balancing, packing, stock 
and goods in transit.
