THE RIGHT UNDER SECTION 100 OF THE COMPANIES ACT, 2013 IS NOT ABSOLUTE AND CANNOT BE USED AS AN 
INSTRUMENT TO DEFEAT AN INVESTIGATION, JUDICIAL ORDERS AND THE POLICY OF THE IBC 
45. That we do not dispute that Section 100 of the Companies Act, 2013 confers upon members holding the requisite 
shareholding a right to requisition an extraordinary general meeting. That right, however, is a statutory right which 
must be exercised bona fide, in accordance with the Act and the rules framed thereunder, and consistently with other 
applicable laws and binding judicial orders. It is settled law that fraud vitiates every solemn act, and a statutory right 
cannot be invoked as a device to achieve an object which the law forbids. 
46. That in the present case, the right is sought to be exercised (i) on the basis of a requisition whose signatures, dating 
and service are seriously disputed; (ii) in the teeth of the subsisting restraint imposed by the Hon'ble NCDRC on any 
action for transfer of the management or control of APIL; (iii) by persons who are themselves undergoing personal 
insolvency resolution proceedings and are subject to the moratorium under the IBC; (iv) to defeat an ongoing forensic 
audit into transactions undertaken during their own tenure; and (v) to restore to control the Promoter Group under 
whose management APIL was admitted into CIRP, contrary to the legislative policy underlying Section 29A of the IBC. 
In such exceptional circumstances, the exercise ceases to be a bona fide exercise of shareholder democracy and 
becomes an abuse of the statutory process. 
47. That the Directors are under a statutory obligation under Section 166(2) of the Companies Act, 2013 to act in good faith 
in the best interests of the Company, its employees, its shareholders and the community. The Independent Directors 
are further bound, under Section 149(8) read with Schedule IV (Code for Independent Directors) of the Companies Act,
