## Executive Overview

Century Textiles and Industries Limited (CTIL) reported a strong Q3 FY2022 performance, driven by robust operational efficiencies and improved market sentiments. Consolidated turnover grew by 38% year-on-year to Rs. 1,050 crores. While consolidated EBITDA saw a marginal decline of 3% to Rs. 105 crores (impacted by an exceptional one-time income in the prior year's Q3), on an adjusted basis, EBITDA grew by 31%. Net profit declined to Rs. 12 crores from Rs. 37 crores in the prior year, also due to the prior year's exceptional income. The Pulp & Paper segment achieved its lifetime highest ever total production of 1,22,888 metric tons, with 110% capacity utilization. The Textile segment saw a turnaround in EBITDA and reached over 95% capacity utilization. The Real Estate division launched Birla Tisya in Bengaluru, achieving over Rs. 250 crores in sales within four days, and also commenced pre-launch outreach for its flagship project, Birla Niyaara at Worli. Management remains optimistic about future performance, anticipating a subsiding COVID-19 third wave and continued growth driven by strong business fundamentals.

## Business Strategy & Drivers

*   **Real Estate:** Focus on launching and selling inventory in key projects like Birla Niyaara (Worli) and new phases of existing projects (NCR, Kalyan). Strong response to Birla Tisya (Bengaluru) launch highlights market demand. Emphasis on leveraging the Birla brand and digital channels. Aggressively pursuing new deals in key metro cities (Mumbai, NCR, Bangalore, Pune).
*   **Pulp & Paper:** Capitalizing on record production and capacity utilization (110%) driven by recovery in writing & printing paper (copier and non-copier) due to office and educational institution reopenings. Robust demand from Pharma, FMCG, and food packaging for the Board segment. Growing consumption of tissue products supported by hygiene awareness.
*   **Textiles:** Strong apparel demand in domestic and international markets, with production increasing by 10% and capacity utilization exceeding 95%. Bed linen segment facing headwinds in the US due to inflation and inventory build-up. Management expects market stability by February.
*   **Sustainability & ESG:** Continued focus on ESG initiatives, including using wood bark as fuel, constructing toilets, developing community parks, and initiating wind power purchase for textile operations.

## Management Accountability & Consistency Audit

*   **Historical Context Audit:**
    *   **Real Estate:** Management's narrative on robust momentum in the real estate sector, driven by market sentiment and low home loan rates, aligns with the strong sales performance of Birla Tisya. The acknowledgement of potential short-term disruptions from the COVID-19 third wave due to labor shortages is consistent with prior concerns about pandemic impacts. The focus on digital channels for continued steady performance is also consistent.
    *   **Pulp & Paper:** Management's observation of record production and capacity utilization aligns with expectations of demand recovery. The impact of sharp increases in raw material and freight costs, and the undertaking of multiple price increases, is consistent with their strategy to balance costs. The outlook for paper and paper products facing a slowdown due to the Omicron variant and delayed government syllabus guidelines is a new development compared to the previous quarter's positive outlook on this segment. However, the continued strong demand for boards from Pharma, FMCG, and food sectors is consistent.
    *   **Textiles:** Management's report of good apparel demand in domestic and international markets is consistent with prior expectations of festive season pick-up. The EBITDA turnaround and production increase are positive outcomes. The significant pressure on the bed linen segment in the US due to inflation and delayed cotton crop arrival leading to unexpected raw material price rises is a new and significant headwind not explicitly detailed in the previous quarter's summary. The expectation of market stability by February is a new forward-looking statement.
    *   **Overall Tone on Headwinds:** Management's tone remains generally optimistic, but they acknowledge increased raw material, logistics, and freight costs as significant headwinds, particularly impacting margins in the paper and textile segments. The explanation for the inability to fully pass on cost increases to customers is more detailed than in the previous quarter.

Expectation is for margins to improve gradually, moving back towards pre-COVID levels. Q4 may still see some impact, but not expected to worsen. Imported pulp impact will be realized after consumption.

    *   **Leasing Business:** Slight dip in leasing income due to one client reducing demand by one floor in Birla Centurion (coworking space). Currently in talks with promising clients for vacant spaces.
    *   **Corporate Structure:** No discussion on demerger or restructuring.
    *   **Overall Outlook:** Confident that coming quarters will be stronger due to robust business fundamentals and market dynamics, assuming the third wave subsides with marginal impact.
