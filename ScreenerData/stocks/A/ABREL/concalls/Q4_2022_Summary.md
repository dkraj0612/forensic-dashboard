## Executive Overview

Century Textiles and Industries Limited (CTIL) reported a strong Q4 FY2022, concluding a year of significant turnaround and growth. Consolidated turnover for FY2022 surged by 58% YoY to Rs. 4,068 crore, driven by a revival in both the paper and textile manufacturing businesses. Consolidated EBITDA for FY2022 grew by 71% YoY to Rs. 487 crore, with a 12% EBITDA margin, and net profit after tax turned around to Rs. 154 crore from a loss of Rs. 15 crore in the previous fiscal. For Q4 FY2022 specifically, consolidated turnover grew 45% YoY to Rs. 1,188 crore, EBITDA increased 65% to Rs. 133 crore, and net profit after tax was Rs. 84 crore, a significant improvement from a loss of Rs. 4 crore in Q4 FY2021. The company's real estate business achieved a major milestone with the successful launch of Birla Niyaara at Worli, Mumbai, generating over Rs. 1,200 crore in sales since launch. The Pulp & Paper segment saw record sales volumes and improved realizations, while the Textile division achieved its highest ever turnover. Management remains optimistic about continued performance, anticipating further growth across all business verticals.

## Business Strategy & Drivers

*   **Real Estate:** Focus on capitalizing on the residential real estate upcycle. Successful launch of Birla Niyaara in Worli with strong sales is a key highlight. Expansion plans include new phases in Kalyan and Gurugram, and a significant new development in North Bengaluru. Strategy involves leveraging the Birla brand and expanding geographical presence in key metro cities.
*   **Pulp & Paper:** Continued outperformance driven by strong demand across segments, including copier and non-copier paper, paperboards for packaging, and tissue products. Operational efficiency is being enhanced through debottlenecking and capacity expansion. Management anticipates a positive medium-term outlook for the Indian paper industry due to market reopening and potential demand from new educational syllabi.
*   **Textiles:** Focus on apparel fabric demand in domestic and international markets. For home textiles, strategy shifts to more price-sensitive products and blending with man-made fibers. Geographical expansion into Australia, Saudi Arabia, and Russia is planned. Management is cautiously monitoring input costs.
*   **Sustainability & ESG:** Continued commitment to ESG initiatives, including seed distribution, waste upcycling, and community development programs.

## Management Accountability & Consistency Audit

*   **Historical Context Audit:**
    *   **Real Estate:** Management's narrative of a robust real estate market and successful project launches aligns with the strong sales figures for Birla Niyaara and Birla Navya. The proactive approach to business development and pipeline expansion mentioned previously is now evident in the new JDA for the North Bengaluru project. Management's expectation of a multi-year upcycle for residential real estate appears to be materializing.
    *   **Pulp & Paper:** The segment's performance in Q4 FY2022 shows a continued turnaround, with strong volumes and improved realizations, which is consistent with management's expectation of demand recovery. However, the earlier concern about sharp increases in raw material and freight costs leading to margin pressure is reiterated. The strategy of undertaking multiple price increases to offset these costs is also consistent. The outlook for the paper industry remains optimistic due to market reopening and government initiatives.
    *   **Textiles:** The EBITDA turnaround and increased sales in the textile segment align with expectations. The previously identified headwind in the US home textile market due to inflation and inventory build-up has materialized as a significant challenge, as detailed by management. The expectation of market stability by February has been revised to "a couple of months" for inventory to clear. The impact of cotton price rises is more acutely felt than previously detailed.
    *   **Overall Tone on Headwinds:** Management continues to acknowledge significant headwinds from raw material, logistics, and freight costs, particularly impacting margins. The tone is one of cautious optimism, highlighting efforts to balance price increases with market absorption and customer interests. The previous narrative of passing on costs is now more nuanced, emphasizing the need for balance and careful assessment of market limits.
