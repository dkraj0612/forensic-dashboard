## Executive Overview

Adani Power Limited (APL) exhibited strong operational resilience in Q4 and FY26, driven by an efficient fleet and enhanced capacity tie-ups, despite subdued power demand growth (0.8% YoY for FY26) attributed to prolonged monsoons. The company reported robust EBITDA of INR 6,498 crores for Q4 FY26 (up 27% YoY) and INR 23,431 crores for FY26. Profit After Tax (PAT) for Q4 FY26 surged 64% YoY to INR 4,271 crores, with full-year PAT at INR 12,971 crores. APL continues its aggressive capacity expansion, securing 10.4 GW under long-term PPAs in FY26, bringing total expansion tie-ups to 13.3 GW. With 95% of its 18.15 GW operating capacity secured under PPAs, the company has significantly reduced market volatility exposure. Diversification into hydro and nuclear power remains on the agenda.

## Business Strategy & Drivers

*   **Capacity Expansion & PPA Focus:** APL is advancing towards its 23.7 GW thermal capacity addition target by FY32. 10.4 GW of expansion capacity was secured under PPAs in FY26, with total tie-ups reaching 13.3 GW. The strategy emphasizes securing high PPA coverage (95% of operating capacity) for revenue visibility and stability, with new PPAs offering improved capacity charges.
*   **Operational Resilience & Demand Recovery:** APL maintained strong operational performance, with higher capacity charges from new PPAs and the contribution of the acquired Butibori plant contributing to resilient EBITDA. Management anticipates improved power demand in FY27.
*   **Diversification & New Avenues:** The company is exploring hydro and nuclear power projects, with an SPV in Bhutan for a hydro plant and identification of new sites for nuclear projects pending government notification.
*   **Financial Management:** Prudent debt management and a focus on self-funded growth with low leverage are highlighted. The issuance of AA-rated NCDs aims to fund capacity expansion and loan repayment.
*   **Fuel Security:** APL benefits from efficient fuel sourcing, with domestic coal supply secured through long-term FSAs (87% of domestic coal-based capacity) and pass-through of imported coal costs via PPAs.
