## Executive Overview

Adani Power Limited (APL) reported a resilient Q1 FY26 performance, characterized by stable revenue and robust EBITDA despite a demand slowdown due to early monsoons. The company successfully integrated the 600 MW Vidarbha Industries Power Ltd. (VIPL) acquisition, expanding its Maharashtra footprint. A significant highlight was the receipt of over US$500 million in outstanding dues from the Bangladesh Power Development Board, normalizing receivables. APL continues to strategically advance its significant capacity expansion plans, underpinned by strong operational capabilities and financial discipline, while emphasizing its long-term conviction in the thermal power sector's role in India's growth.

## Business Strategy & Drivers

*   **Operational Resilience:** Despite weather-induced demand softness and a high base from Q1 FY25, APL achieved robust power dispatch (24.6 billion units, +1.6% YoY).
*   **Capacity Expansion & Integration:** Successfully acquired and revived the 600 MW Vidarbha Industries Power Ltd. (VIPL), expanding presence in Maharashtra. Amalgamation of Adani Power (Jharkhand) Limited (Godda plant) into APL's standalone entity completed, leading to implicit credit rating upgrade.
*   **Revenue & Tariff Dynamics:** Revenue remained stable sequentially, though YoY revenue was lower due to reduced import coal prices and softer merchant tariffs. Operating expenses increased due to recent acquisitions.
*   **Strategic PPA Wins:** Signed a long-term PPA with Uttar Pradesh Discom for 1,600 MW capacity at a capacity charge of 3.73 and total tariff of 5.39. Actively bidding for large PPAs in Bihar, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, and Uttarakhand.
*   **Financial Prudence:** Maintained tight control over finance costs despite acquisitions. Debt levels have increased due to acquisitions and interim bridge funding but are expected to be managed by robust internal accruals. Emphasis on self-funded, debt-light growth.
*   **Long-Term Outlook:** Strong conviction in the thermal power sector's continued importance for India's economic growth, anticipating future power deficits requiring significant thermal capacity addition.
