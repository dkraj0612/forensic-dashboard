## Executive Overview

Adani Power Limited (APL) delivered another strong quarter in Q2 FY25, demonstrating robust growth and operational excellence, further solidifying its position as India's largest private thermal power producer. The company has significantly expanded its operational capacity to 17,550 MW as of September 2024, through the strategic acquisition of 2,300 MW from Adani Dahanu, Lanco Amarkantak (now Korba Power), and Coastal Energen. This expansion is part of an aggressive growth journey aimed at achieving 30+ GW of capacity by FY30, supported by securing long-term power supply agreements (PSAs).

APL is strategically positioned to benefit from India's rapidly growing electricity demand, with peak power demand projected to reach 388 GW by FY32. The government's increased thermal power capacity addition target of 80 GW by FY32 underscores the essential role of thermal power for base load, peak supply, and grid balancing. The company's core competencies in plant operation, fuel management, and logistics are being leveraged to turn around newly acquired stressed assets and improve their efficiency.

Financially, APL continues its deleveraging strategy, with regulatory recovery proceeds utilized judiciously to reduce debt per megawatt and improve its credit rating to "AA/Stable" by CARE. The company emphasizes a "debt-light" strategy, relying on strong cash flow generation from existing operations to fund its substantial capital expenditure plans, which include advance orders for 11.2 GW of capacity. This disciplined approach, coupled with a focus on project execution and cost optimization, underpins APL's commitment to creating long-term value for its stakeholders.

## Business Strategy & Drivers

*   **Aggressive Capacity Expansion:** APL added 2,300 MW through acquisitions, increasing its operational capacity to 17,550 MW. This is part of a larger organic capacity addition target of 12.5 GW and an overall ambition to reach **30+ GW by FY30**.
*   **Securing Long-Term PPAs:** The company secured a Letter of Award for 1,496 MW power supply to Maharashtra DISCOM under a 25-year PPA, originating from a new 1,600 MW Ultra-Supercritical Thermal Power Project (U-SCTPP) in Chhattisgarh. This contributes to 3 GW of long-term tie-ups out of the 12.5 GW organic target.
*   **Stressed Asset Turnaround:** APL is committed to rapidly turning around newly acquired stressed power plants (e.g., Korba Power Limited, Coastal Energen) by leveraging its expertise in plant operations, maintenance, fuel management, logistics, and power sales management.
*   **Capitalizing on Market Demand:** India's rapidly increasing electricity demand, with government projections for significant thermal power capacity additions, creates a favorable market for APL's growth in both long-term and short-term (merchant) markets.
*   **Financial Prudence & Deleveraging:** The company continues to prioritize a "debt-light" strategy, utilizing strong internal cash flows to fund substantial capital expenditures and deleverage its balance sheet, as evidenced by improved credit ratings and reduced debt per megawatt.
*   **De-risked Project Execution:** APL has placed advance orders for 11.2 GW of capacity from its Main Plant Supplier, including BTG orders to BHEL, to ensure timely equipment availability and mitigate project execution risks for phased commissioning by FY30.
*   **Ongoing Project Development:** Significant projects are underway, including the 1,600 MW U-SCTPP expansion at Mahan Energen Site (Singrauli, MP), and preparatory work has commenced for projects in Raipur and Raigarh, alongside development for Kawai, Mahan, and Mirzapur.

## Management Accountability & Consistency Audit

**Historical Context (Q1 FY25):** The previous summary highlighted APL's operational capacity of 15.25 GW, 1.60 GW under construction, and a total planned capacity target of 22.08 GW. Key financial metrics included a Net Debt / Continuing EBITDA of 1.22x (June 30, 2024) and a focus on operational excellence, deleveraging, and ESG initiatives (e.g., water intensity, ash utilization, GHG target). New projects like Raipur Phase-II and Mirzapur greenfield were initiated, and the MEL/SMRPL amalgamation was in process.

**Audit:**
*   **Capacity Expansion:** Management has *exceeded* the Q1 FY25 operational capacity of 15.25 GW, reaching 17.55 GW by Q2 FY25 through significant acquisitions. This demonstrates strong execution on their capacity expansion strategy.
*   **Planned Capacity Target:** The previous "total planned capacity" target of 22.08 GW has been *updated and significantly expanded* to an overarching ambition of **30+ GW by FY30**, indicating increased ambition and confidence in market demand.
*   **Deleveraging:** The narrative on deleveraging remains consistent and positive. While a specific Net Debt/EBITDA figure for Q2 FY25 was not provided, management affirmed that "debt per megawatt has come down significantly" and highlighted the improved "AA/Stable" credit rating, aligning with the stated financial discipline.
*   **EBITDA Growth:** Continuing EBITDA growth remains robust, with a 24.6% YoY increase in Q2 FY25 and 38.3% in H1 FY25, building on the Q1 FY25 reported growth. This confirms the positive trend driven by higher dispatches and improved margins.
*   **Regulatory Income:** The expectation of lower one-time regulatory income recognition due to resolution of past issues, as noted in Q1 FY25, has *materialized*. Q2 FY25 reported significantly lower one-time prior period revenue, validating this aspect of the previous narrative.
*   **New Project Development:** The initiated projects (Raipur Phase-II, Mirzapur) mentioned in Q1 FY25 are progressing, with management confirming orders for BTG equipment and preparatory work. Mahan Phase 2 is specifically noted as "under execution," showing tangible progress on expansion plans.
*   **Headwinds/Tone Shift:** There is no material shift in management's narrative or tone regarding headwinds. Optimism about market demand and APL's competitive positioning remains high. Bangladesh receivables continue to be a focus, but management expresses confidence in collections, supported by recent payment receipts, suggesting a controlled situation rather than a significant new headwind.
