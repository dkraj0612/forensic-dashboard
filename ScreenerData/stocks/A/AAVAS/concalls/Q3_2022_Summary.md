## Executive Overview

Aavas Financiers Limited (AAVAS) demonstrated robust performance in Q3 FY22, navigating a milder third wave of COVID-19 with minimal impact on business and collections. The company reported strong year-over-year growth in disbursements (24%) and Assets Under Management (AUM) (20%). A significant achievement was the sharp improvement in asset quality, with 1+ Days Past Due (DPD) notably reducing to 6.45% in December 2021 (and further to ~6.25% in January 2022), surpassing its Mar-21 levels ahead of schedule. However, the implementation of RBI's November 12, 2021, notification harmonizing IRACP norms led to a reclassification of some up to 90 DPD assets as Gross Non-Performing Assets (GNPA), resulting in a Gross Stage 3 of 1.72% (which has since reduced to ~1.5% in Jan-22). The company's long-term credit rating outlook was revised to AA- / Positive by ICRA, reflecting its strong financial health. Management maintains a cautious yet consistent growth approach, prioritizing asset quality and operational efficiency.

## Business Strategy & Drivers

*   **Consistent Growth & Quality:** Continues a cautious growth approach, prioritizing pristine asset quality and sustained operating metrics. Aims for consistency rather than aggressive expansion.
*   **Customer Focus:** Home loans remain dominant (71.6%), with other mortgage loans at 28.4%. Customer mix remains 39.7% salaried and 60.3% self-employed. New states initially see more salaried business.
*   **Network Expansion:** Maintained branch network expansion, adding 35 branches in the last 12 months to reach 298. New branches support future growth capacity.
*   **Risk Management:** Maintains a prudent approach with zero exposure to commercial paper. Aggressively managed asset quality, leading to significant DPD reduction despite the RBI NPA circular.
*   **Funding Diversification:** Leverages diversified and cost-effective long-term financing, with incremental borrowings at competitive rates (5.91% in FY22).
*   **Operational Efficiency & Technology:** Reinvesting in business capacity and manpower for future growth. Exploring alternate channels (e.g., Aavas Mitra App, digital sourcing) for lead generation, aiming to increase their share of business.

## Management Accountability & Consistency Audit

### Performance Against Stated Targets (Q1 FY2022 Context):

*   **AUM Growth:** Achieved 20% YoY growth (Dec-21), aligning with previous indications of sustained growth (21% in Q1 FY22). **Consistent.**
*   **PAT Growth:** Reported strong YoY growth for 9M FY22 (19% Ind-AS, 17% IGAAP), indicating continued profitability. This is consistent with Q1 FY22's 20% Ind-AS and 19% IGAAP growth. **Consistent.**
*   **Asset Quality (1+DPD):** Q1 FY22 saw a significant deterioration to 12.67%, with management expecting gradual reduction in the next two to three quarters to normalize towards previous levels (Mar-21: 6.37%). Current: 1+DPD notably improved to 6.45% in Dec-21 and further to ~6.25% in Jan-22, effectively reaching/surpassing the Mar-21 level *ahead* of the anticipated Mar-22 timeline. **Exceeded expectations.**
*   **ROA/ROE:** Q1 FY22 ROA was 2.64% and ROE 9.84%. Current: 9M FY22 ROA 3.37%, ROE 12.70% (Q3 FY22 ROA 3.58%, ROE 13.62%). These figures represent a significant improvement over Q1 FY22, demonstrating a strong recovery in profitability. **Exceeded expectations.**
*   **Spread:** Maintained a healthy spread of 5.76% (Dec-21), consistent with Q1 FY22's 5.74%. **Consistent.**
*   **Disbursements:** Q1 FY22 reported 117% YoY growth. Current: 24% YoY growth for Q3 FY22 and 41% YoY for 9M FY22. While the Q1 YoY growth was high due to a low base, the current figures reflect sustained strong business momentum in line with the "consistent player" approach. **Consistent.**

### Narrative/Tone Shift & Headwinds:

*   **COVID-19 Impact:** The narrative shifted from acknowledging a "severe impact" of the second wave (Q1 FY22) to a more confident tone regarding the third wave, stating "symptoms were milder as compared to prior waves. Curbs... were largely restricted to weekend & night curfews. Consequently, the impact on business & collections was relatively very low." This indicates a significantly less severe impact than previously experienced.
*   **Asset Quality & RBI Circular:** Management's tone on 1+DPD improvement is highly positive, highlighting achievement of Mar-21 levels ahead of schedule. However, a new reporting headwind emerged with the RBI notification dated November 12, 2021, which required harmonizing IRACP norms. This led to a reclassification of 0.89% of up to 90 DPD assets as GNPA, increasing the reported Gross Stage 3. Management explicitly addressed this, including the provisioning and interest reversal impact, demonstrating transparency.

### New Forward-Looking Promises, Guidance, Margins, or Timelines:

*   **Disbursement Growth:** YTD January FY22 disbursements have already crossed full year FY21. Anticipates 20-25% growth YoY for the business.
*   **Asset Quality (1+DPD/GS3):** 1+DPD further reduced to ~6.25% in Jan-22. Gross Stage 3 (including RBI impact) reduced by 20 bps in Jan-22 to around the 1.5% range. Management expects continued better performance on collections.
*   **Opex & Operating Leverage:** While Opex-to-ATA temporarily increased due to COVID impacts and investments, management expects a 25-30 bps decrease YoY in the near-term (next 2-3 quarters), and operating leverage to start showing in the next 12-18 months.
*   **Branch Expansion:** Will add around 34-35 branches this year, reaching 314-315 branches, maintaining the strategy of 30-40 branches annually.
*   **Spread:** Plans to continue maintaining a ~5% spread. Any excess spread will be shifted to customers if COVID situation improves.
*   **ROA/ROE:** Guided for a sustainable 2.5%-2.6% ROA on a 6-7 times loan book to capital leverage. Expects ROE to reach 15-16% in the near-term once COVID provisioning goes off.
*   **Credit Cost:** Expects sustainable credit cost around 0.5%.
*   **Alternate Sourcing Channels:** Current alternate channel sourcing (e.g., Aavas Mitra App, digital) is 6-7% of total business, with an intention to increase it to 15% in the next two to three years. DSA contribution remains low (~1% or less).
*   **AUM Mix:** Expects to be around 72:28 or 70:30 for home loans to other mortgage loans this quarter.
*   **Subsidiary Investment:** Investment was made to meet the increased minimum capital requirement (Rs. 10 Crores) for the NBFC license.
*   **Write-offs:** Negligible for Q3 FY22 (~Rs. 1.9 Crores for 9M FY22).
