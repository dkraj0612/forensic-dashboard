## Executive Overview

Aavas Financiers Limited (AAVAS) navigated the challenges of the second COVID-19 wave in Q1 FY22, demonstrating operational resilience. While business was impacted in the first half of the quarter due to localized lockdowns, a phased reopening in the latter half saw a recovery. The company achieved significant year-over-year growth in disbursements (117%) and Assets Under Management (AUM) (21%), while maintaining operating metrics and reporting Profit After Tax (PAT) growth of 20% (Ind-AS) and 19% (IGAAP). A key concern is the rise in 1+ Days Past Due (DPD) to 12.67% from 6.37% in the previous quarter, a trend management anticipates will reverse with economic recovery, drawing parallels to post-demonetization trends. The company continues its strategy of serving its core customer segment in semi-urban and rural areas, with home loans forming the bulk of its portfolio.

## Business Strategy & Drivers

*   **Customer Focus:** Continues to serve low- and middle-income customers in semi-urban and rural areas. Home loans remain the dominant product (72.7%), with Other Mortgage Loans at 27.3%. Salaried customers comprise 39.7% and self-employed 60.3%.
*   **Network Expansion:** Accelerated branch network expansion, adding 33 branches in the last 12 months to reach 284. Plans to continue this growth trajectory by opening 30-40 branches annually, with a strategy to penetrate new states cautiously.
*   **Risk Management:** Maintains a prudent approach with zero exposure to commercial papers. Focus on enhanced underwriting practices. Management acknowledges increased 1+DPD due to COVID-19 but remains hopeful for a reversal based on past experience. A cautious approach to new markets (e.g., Odisha) is maintained.
*   **Funding Diversification:** Access to diversified and cost-effective long-term financing. Borrowings include term loans, assignments, NHB, and debt capital markets. Incremental borrowing costs are competitive, with management guiding for an average cost of borrowing around 7.25% for the foreseeable future.
*   **Operational Resilience & Efficiency:** Emphasis on improving customer experience through technology and process improvements, aiming to reduce turnaround time for loan processing.

## Management Accountability & Consistency Audit

### Performance Against Stated Targets (Q1 FY2022):

*   **AUM Growth:** Achieved 21% YoY growth, aligning with previous indications of sustained growth.
*   **PAT Growth:** Reported strong YoY growth for Q1 FY22 (20% Ind-AS, 19% IGAAP), indicating continued profitability.
*   **Asset Quality (1+DPD):** Experienced a significant deterioration with 1+DPD rising to 12.67% from 6.37% in Q4 FY21. This is a deviation from the expected improvement trajectory and a key area of concern.
*   **ROA/ROE:** Achieved ROA of 2.64% and ROE of 9.84% in Q1 FY22. These are lower than the previous quarter's robust figures, likely impacted by the rise in DPD and increased provisioning.
*   **Spread:** Maintained a healthy spread of 5.74%, consistent with previous performance.
*   **Disbursements:** Achieved a significant 117% YoY growth in disbursements, hitting Rs. 462.5 Crores, demonstrating strong business momentum in the latter half of the quarter.

### Narrative/Tone Shift & Headwinds:

*   **COVID-19 Impact:** Management acknowledged the severe impact of the second COVID-19 wave on business in the first half of the quarter, leading to a rise in 1+DPD. The tone shifted from optimism about further reduction in DPD (as seen in Q4 FY21 call) to cautious hope for a gradual reduction, contingent on economic recovery and no third wave.
*   **Moratorium & Collections:** The narrative highlighted that while no nationwide moratorium was imposed, some customers missed installments due to localized lockdowns. The management draws a parallel to demonetization in terms of expected recovery trends for 1+DPD. Collection efficiency for June was noted at around 98-99%.
*   **Economic Environment:** Confidence in the affordable housing segment remains, but the immediate impact of localized lockdowns on borrower repayment capacity was evident in the 1+DPD numbers.
*   **Funding Environment:** Competitive borrowing costs continue, with management highlighting NHB funding at attractive rates, though with potential caps on onward lending.

### New Forward-Looking Promises, Guidance, Margins, or Timelines:

*   **1+DPD Normalization:** Management expects 1+DPD to gradually reduce in the next two to three quarters, provided there is no third wave and the economy continues to improve. They anticipate normalization towards previous levels.
*   **Restructuring:** Approximately Rs. 114.96 Crores restructured, impacting 1400 customers. Management anticipates the final percentage of restructuring to be less than the initially projected 2% to 2.5%.
*   **Yield Pressure:** Base rate was reduced by 15 bps from April 1, 2021, impacting yields.
*   **Branch Expansion:** Continued strategy to open 30-40 branches annually, with a phased entry into new states and deeper penetration in existing ones.
*   **ECL Provisioning:** Management is confident with the current ECL provision at 0.85% (Rs. 66 Crores total, including Rs. 31 Crores for COVID Phase 1 & restructured accounts) and does not foresee major changes, maintaining it as a buffer.
*   **Turnaround Time:** Continued focus on reducing loan processing turnaround time, aiming for 1-2 days for repeat/existing customers and progressively reducing it for new customers.
*   **Loan Tenor:** Average portfolio tenure is around 14 years door-to-door, with a behavioral tenure of 7.5 to 8 years.
*   **AUM Mix:** Aiming to maintain the housing loan share in the bracket of 73-75%, with a corresponding increase in non-home loans.
