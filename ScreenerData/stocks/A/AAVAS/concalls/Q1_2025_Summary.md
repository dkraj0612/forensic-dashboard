## Executive Overview

Aavas Financiers Limited (AAVAS) reported Q1 FY'25 results showcasing strong momentum with a 22% YoY growth in Assets Under Management (AUM) to INR 178 billion, reinforcing their FY'25 guidance of 20-25% growth. Disbursements saw a 13% YoY increase to INR 12.11 billion, supported by a significant 25% YoY rise in sanctions, indicating healthy future pipeline. Net profit grew by 15% YoY to INR 1.26 billion. Asset quality remains a core strength, with 1+DPD at 3.65% (a 3 bps YoY improvement) and GNPA at 1.01%. Operational efficiency improved with Opex to Assets ratio decreasing by 52 bps YoY to 3.27%. The company is progressing on technology adoption, with Salesforce stabilized and a new lead management system piloted. Incremental cost of borrowing rose to 8.31%, though management expects it to stabilize. Aavas is exploring co-lending partnerships and maintaining its focus on self-employed individuals in Tier 2 to Tier 5 towns.

## Business Strategy & Drivers

*   **Technology Adoption:** Salesforce (LOS) adoption stabilized, processing 1.9 lakh+ loan applications. Oracle Fusion (ERP) stabilized. A new lead management system (Salesforce-based) is in pilot. Oracle FLEXCUBE (LMS) is in advanced testing for Q2 FY'25 launch. Login-to-sanction turnaround time improved to 8 days.
*   **Distribution Network:** Four new branches opened in Q1 FY'25 in existing states to deepen market penetration. Diversified omnichannel lead generation (digital, e-Mitra, RRO, Mitra) is driving strong logins.
*   **Product Strategy:** Shift in "other mortgage" from LAP/top-ups to MSME loans for working capital. Continued focus on self-occupied residential properties and smaller ticket sizes (<INR 15 lakhs) to maintain spreads.
*   **Liability Management:** Exploring co-lending tie-ups, with progress made on a PSU bank partnership. Borrowing mix is designed to benefit from potential rate cuts.
*   **Asset Quality Maintenance:** Disciplined underwriting and detailed geographical and ticket-size segmentation analysis are key to maintaining industry-leading asset quality.
*   **Government Initiatives:** Positive outlook on PMAY and 'Housing for All' initiatives, anticipating their impact on affordability and loan quality.

## Management Accountability & Consistency Audit

### Performance Against Stated Targets & Narrative Audit:

*   **AUM Growth:** Achieved 22% YoY AUM growth, meeting the lower end of the 20-25% FY'25 guidance. The narrative remains consistent with a growth-oriented strategy.
*   **Disbursements:** Reported 13% YoY disbursement growth. However, the sanction-to-disbursement ratio at 77% was lower than the historical 85-87% range, leading to muted disbursements. Management attributed this to improved disbursement practices following a regulatory circular and a "lower run rate" with expected normalization in coming months. This is a shift from the previous quarter's emphasis on an accounting change.
*   **Asset Quality:** Maintained strong asset quality with 1+DPD improving YoY and GNPA remaining low. The narrative remains consistent, emphasizing pristine asset quality.
*   **Profitability:** Net profit growth of 15% YoY demonstrates consistent profitability.
*   **Opex to Assets Ratio:** Achieved significant improvement, down 52 bps YoY to 3.27%, progressing towards the long-term target of around 3%.
*   **Borrowing Costs:** Incremental borrowing costs increased, but management believes the peak has been reached, expecting benefits from rate cuts. The overall cost of borrowing saw a marginal increase. The narrative suggests proactive liability management.
*   **Spread Maintenance:** Maintained a 5% spread despite competitive pressures and rising incremental borrowing costs, attributed to product mix adjustments and Opex leverage.
*   **BT-out:** Management reiterated BT-out rates remain controlled within the 5-5.5% range, consistent with previous statements.

*   **AUM Growth Guidance:** Confirmed 20-25% AUM growth guidance for FY'25.
*   **Disbursement Recovery:** Confident in disbursement rebound in upcoming quarters due to strong sanction growth and expected normalization of the sanction-to-disbursement ratio. Positive disbursement momentum expected for July, August, and September.
