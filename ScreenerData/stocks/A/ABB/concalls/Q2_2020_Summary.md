## Executive Overview

ABB India reported a Q2 2020 performance heavily impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic and associated lockdowns, leading to a significant decline in orders and revenues compared to the previous year. Despite these headwinds, management highlighted the company's ability to sustain business continuity through remote services and digitalization. A strong focus on health and safety, cash and cost management, and disciplined operational execution was emphasized. While the quarter saw a substantial drop in PBT, management expressed optimism about the velocity of business picking up in July and the resilience of its core product segments. The company continues to navigate challenges by prioritizing critical investments and leveraging its diversified market presence.

## Key Financial Metrics

*   **Orders:**
    *   Q2 2020: INR 1,200 crore (-40% YoY)
    *   *Note:* This figure reflects approximately 45 working days due to lockdowns.
    *   *Solar Impact (for normalization):* Q2 2019 orders included INR 240 crore from solar.
*   **Revenue:**
    *   Q2 2020: Not explicitly stated in INR, but reported down 43% YoY (normalized for solar).
    *   *Note:* Revenue benefited from pent-up demand from March.
*   **Profitability:**
    *   Q2 2020 PBT: INR 23 crore (reported, including INR 2 crore exceptional item), down ~80% YoY (from INR 113 crore in Q2 2019).
    *   *Solar Impact (for normalization):* Q2 2019 revenue included INR 139 crore from solar.
    *   Q2 2020 effective tax rate (ETR): 25%-26%.
*   **Order Backlog:**
    *   End of Q2 2020: INR 4,671 crore (steady from previous quarters).
*   **Cash Position:**
    *   End of Q2 2020: INR 1,500 crore (cash balance).
*   **Other Metrics:**
    *   *Forex Impact:* Q2 2020 saw a Forex gain of INR 34 crore, partially offsetting prior quarter losses.
    *   *Net Working Capital:* Adverse movement noted, primarily due to non-novated contracts from the Power Grid (PG) business.
    *   *Government/PSU vs. Private Sector:* Estimated ~10% of revenue/order book from government/PSU (including railways, utilities); the balance from industry and exports.

## Business Strategy & Drivers

*   **COVID-19 Response:** Prioritizing health and safety, sustaining business continuity through remote services and digitalization, and managing cash and cost effectively.
*   **Digitalization & Remote Services:** Extensive use of virtual tools, remote logging-in techniques, and offering cloud services for free to customers to maintain business continuity and demonstrate digital capabilities.
*   **Market Segment Focus:** Concentrating resources on green and yellow segments (those with good movement), including data centers, metros, water, automotive (for Electrification), transportation (railways, metro), digitalization, and services (for Motion).
*   **Industrial Automation & Robotics:** Acknowledging challenges but continuing investments due to long-term growth potential. Focus on Food & Beverage (F&B) and Electronics sectors for Robotics.
*   **Services and Exports:** Expanding services business leveraging a strong installed base and customer loyalty. Group is increasingly leveraging India's competitive manufacturing base for global exports.
*   **Customer Engagement:** Conducting co-discovery workshops and customer interactions using virtual tools.

## Management Accountability & Consistency Audit

**Stated Targets vs. Actual Performance:**

*   **Resilience and Predictable Performance:** Management emphasized prioritizing health, safety, business continuity, and cash/cost management to deliver "predictable performance." The operational ramp-up post-lockdown and ability to serve customers remotely indicate a degree of success in maintaining continuity, though financial results were significantly impacted by external factors.
*   **Revenue and Order Growth:** The narrative shifted from growth to managing severe declines. While May, June, and July showed sequential improvement in "velocity" and "run rate" compared to 2019 for operating days, overall Q2 YoY figures showed significant drops (-40% orders, -43% revenue normalized). This is attributed directly to lost working days due to lockdowns rather than a failure in strategy.
*   **Profitability Improvement:** PBT saw a substantial YoY decline of ~80%. Management attributed this to lost working days and a subdued order intake, rather than operational inefficiencies. The favorable Forex gain provided some mitigation.
*   **Digitalization Drive:** The company actively leveraged digital tools and remote services, which aligned with stated strategies and helped maintain customer service during restrictions.
*   **Exports and Services Growth:** While services and exports were impacted by movement restrictions and global supply chain issues, management reiterated their long-term importance and the group's intent to leverage India's capabilities.
*   **Headwinds Acknowledgment:** Management candidly acknowledged the severe impact of lockdowns and uncertainties on orders and revenues. The tone was one of navigating unprecedented challenges rather than failing to meet pre-pandemic targets. The focus was on adapting to the current reality.

**New Forward-Looking Promises, Guidance, Margins, or Timelines:**

*   **Business Velocity:** Expectation of improved velocity and run rate in Q3 and Q4 2020, assuming no further pandemic impact.
*   **Order Backlog Execution:** The order backlog of INR 4,671 crore provides visibility for execution in Q3/Q4 2020 and some into Q1 2021, contingent on market opening and customer/supplier stability.
*   **Cash Position:** Commitment to maintaining a debt-free status with sufficient cash for operational obligations.
*   **Investment Focus:** Continued critical business investments with direct customer impact and revenue acceleration. CAPEX rationalization is in place.
*   **Industrial Automation (IA) & Robotics Margins:** Management aims to bridge the margin gap by increasing utilization rates and diversifying the export mix. The parent company's strategy of achieving 15% EBITDA margin globally sets an ambition for all units.
*   **Forex Impact:** Acknowledged as a factor influencing profitability, with a gain in Q2 2020.
*   **Post-COVID Trends:** Anticipation of consolidation in the market, increased investments in productivity, energy efficiency, Industry 4.0, Robotics, and Automation. Growth expected in data centers, electronics manufacturing, F&B, and pharma sectors.
*   **Services and Exports:** Services expected to pick back up quickly. Geopolitical situations may open new export opportunities, primarily for products, leveraging India's competence and scale.
*   **Building Automation & Warehousing:** Identified as focus areas, with existing technology in building automation (e.g., Delhi airport, hotel chains) and growth potential in warehousing due to e-commerce and GST.
*   **PG Business Novation:** Anomaly in net working capital will persist until PG contracts are fully novated, a lengthy process involving government entities.
*   **IT Restructuring for PG:** Resizing of the IT setup for PG is ongoing and expected to take a minimum of two years, after which costs will move to PG.
*   **Gross Margins:** Aim to maintain current levels, with focus on material costs (~65%) and fine-tuning other expenses. Future margins will depend on market opening and stabilization of costs.
*   **New Orders Contribution:** A mixed bag, with digital/smart products offering better margins and large system orders potentially having lower contribution margins.
*   **Government/PSU Exposure:** Approximately 10% of revenue/order book.
*   **Global CEO's EBITDA Target:** Ambition to achieve 15% EBITDA margin globally, with corresponding effects on units. No specific timeline beyond December 2020 provided due to pandemic uncertainty.
