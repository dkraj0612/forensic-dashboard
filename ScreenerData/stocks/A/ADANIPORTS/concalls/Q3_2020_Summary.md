## Executive Overview

Adani Ports and Special Economic Zone (APSEZ) reported strong performance for the 9 months ended December 2019, demonstrating cargo volume growth of 8% year-on-year, significantly outperforming the all-India average of 5%. Container volumes also grew by 8%, against an all-India average of 4%. This consistent outperformance across operating ports highlights the company's resilience. For the 9-month period, operating revenues grew by 14% and consolidated EBITDA by 15%, with EBITDA margins expanding by 100 basis points to 66%. Port revenue and EBITDA grew by 11%, with port EBITDA margins maintained at 70%. The company is on track to complete the Krishnapatnam acquisition in Q1 FY21, funded through internal accruals and existing cash balances. New terminals at Mundra (LNG) commenced operations in January 2020, and LPG handling is also ramping up. Dhamra Port registered robust 44% cargo volume growth in the 9-month period, surpassing full-year FY19 volumes, supported by improved rail availability and diversification into new cargo types. Adani Logistics Limited (ALL) saw substantial growth in rail volumes (111%) and revenue (56%), with significantly improved EBITDA margins. APSEZ expects to achieve cargo volumes of 224-226 million metric tons for FY20, with revenue and EBITDA growth around 13-14%, and cash flow from operations projected between Rs. 2,250 crores.

## Business Strategy & Drivers

*   **Market Share Expansion:** Continued outperformance in cargo and container volume growth compared to the all-India average, indicating sustained market share gains.
*   **Strategic Acquisitions:** Krishnapatnam acquisition on track for Q1 FY21 completion. Dighi Port acquisition proceedings are underway at NCLT. Snowman Logistics promoter stake acquisition expected by February end, with full closure by March end.
*   **Operational Enhancements:** Mundra LNG terminal (5 MMTPA capacity) commenced operations in January 2020. Mundra LPG handling is increasing, with agreements with HPCL and BPCL expected to drive 1.5 MMT volumes in FY21. Dhamra Port is diversifying cargo streams (gypsum, fertilizer, clinker).
*   **Logistics Network Expansion:** Adani Logistics Limited (ALL) on track to operate 60+ rakes by end of FY20 (currently operating 56). Malur Logistics Park commissioned in Q3 FY20, bringing total to 5. Nagpur logistics park and warehouses at Kattupalli, Taloja, and Mundra to be commissioned in H1 FY21. Adani Agri Logistics Limited (AALL) on track to handle 1.3 MMT cargo in FY20, with 8 Silo units under implementation and participation in UP government tenders for new locations.
*   **Cargo Diversification:** Focus on handling various cargo types at Dhamra and Mundra to reduce reliance on single commodities.
*   **ESG Focus:** Continued focus on operational efficiency and growth, with construction on Myanmar port phase 1 on track for FY22 completion.

## Management Accountability & Consistency Audit

*   **Cargo Volume Growth:** Management consistently guided for and achieved cargo volume growth (8% YoY for 9M FY20) outperforming the all-India average (5%), validating their strategy. The FY20 full-year guidance of 224-226 MMT is maintained with confidence.
*   **Market Share:** The narrative of outperforming all-India growth in both cargo and containers was substantiated with clear figures.
*   **Port EBITDA Margins:** Management successfully maintained 70% port EBITDA margins, demonstrating consistent operational efficiency.
*   **Logistics Performance:** Significant YoY growth in ALL's rail volumes (111%) and revenue (56%), with a substantial margin expansion to 27%, confirms the positive outlook and execution in the logistics segment.
*   **Acquisitions:** Progress on Krishnapatnam, Dighi, and Snowman Logistics acquisitions aligns with stated plans and timelines.
*   **Financial Management:** Management reiterated commitment to maintaining investment-grade ratings and leverage ratios (debt-to-EBITDA target of 3x-3.5x), even with the planned Krishnapatnam acquisition. They indicated that debt levels remain in a similar range as September. The increased depreciation and interest costs were explained by asset capitalization and debt levels.
*   **SEZ Income:** The higher SEZ income in Q3 FY20 was attributed to a fair value gain from an agreement with Total relating to infrastructure development, which is a one-time recognition. The full-year SEZ income guidance of Rs. 800-1,000 crore for FY20 is reiterated.
*   **Crude Oil Impact:** Management acknowledged the impact of lower crude volumes at Mundra due to reduced HPCL and IOCL imports, but expressed confidence in meeting overall cargo guidance by year-end.
*   **Coronavirus Outbreak:** Management stated that as of the call, no impact from the coronavirus outbreak on container volumes or trade originating from China has been observed, with less than 4% of container volumes directly originating from China. Guidance for March has taken this into account.
*   **Dahej Port:** Management views Dahej and Hazira as fungible for bulk cargo, focusing on profitability and managing cargo flow without additional CAPEX. Dahej is expected to handle around 9-10 million tons annually.
