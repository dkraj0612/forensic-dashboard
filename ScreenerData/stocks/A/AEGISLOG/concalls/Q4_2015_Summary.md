## Executive Overview

Aegis Logistics Limited's Q4 2015 earnings transcript highlights a strong focus on expanding its port-based infrastructure and distribution network within India's oil and gas sector, with a particular emphasis on LPG logistics. The company is progressing with its vertical integration strategy, notably through its joint venture with ITOCHU, aimed at establishing Aegis as a leading LPG sourcing player and achieving cost leadership through reduced delivered LPG prices. Significant investments in terminaling capacities at key ports like Haldia, Kandla, and Mumbai, along with pipeline connectivity and a growing retail auto gas station network, underscore the company's expansionary agenda. Management expresses confidence in future growth driven by increased throughput volumes, new infrastructure commissioning, and strategic partnerships.

## Key Financial Metrics

*   **Revenue:** Demonstrated significant year-on-year growth of 28%.
*   **Normalized EBITDA:** Explicitly stated as "Before Forex Hedging Related expenses".
*   **Net Debt to Equity:** Reported at a low 0.08x as of September 2015, indicating a strong financial leverage position.
*   **Profit After Tax (PAT):** Grew by 30% year-on-year.
*   **Dividend:** A 30% interim dividend was declared for 9M FY 2016.

## Business Strategy & Drivers

*   **Vertical Integration & LPG Sourcing:** The formation of a Joint Venture with ITOCHU Petroleum Co. (Singapore) Pte Ltd. by selling a 40% stake in Aegis Group International Pte. Ltd. (AGI) for $5.85 million is a cornerstone of the strategy. This JV aims to position AGI as a leading LPG sourcing entity in India and achieve cost leadership by lowering delivered prices.
*   **Port Infrastructure & Distribution Network Expansion:**
    *   **Terminaling Capacity:** Significant expansions are underway or planned. Kandla is expanding by 100,000 KL (Brownfield), and Haldia is expanding by 25,000 KL (Greenfield). Total capacity post-expansion at Kandla and Haldia is projected to reach approximately 630,000 KL. Existing capacities at Mumbai (120,120 KL), Pipavav (51,000 KL), Haldia (273,000 KL), and Kandla (60,190 KL) are detailed. The company also possesses a capacity of 312,800 MT at its owned terminals, with potential to handle 800,000 MT per annum post-expansion.
    *   **Pipeline Connectivity:** Investments include internal pipeline additions and connectivity for the Mumbai Terminal, with petroleum rail connectivity at Pipavav.
    *   **Throughput Capacity:** The company highlights its ability to handle 0.85 million MT per annum at Mumbai and Pipavav ports. Future plans include building new refrigerated LPG terminals.
    *   **Retail and Bulk Distribution:**
        *   **Auto Gas Retailing:** A network of 102 auto gas stations across 7 states is in operation.
        *   **Bulk Industrial Distribution:** Distribution of LPG through road tankers to various industries, and packed cylinders to commercial users via a network of 76 commercial distributors across 42 cities in 7 states.
*   **Strategic Partnerships:** The JV with ITOCHU for LPG sourcing is a key strategic move. An agreement with ESSAR is also noted, allowing for the sale of ESSAR branded petrol and diesel at up to 60 existing auto gas stations and potentially new sites.
*   **Market Opportunity:** Management identifies a significant opportunity due to constrained domestic LPG supply, growing import reliance, inadequate import infrastructure, and high logistics costs in India.
